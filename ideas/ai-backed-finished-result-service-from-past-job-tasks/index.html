Selling a Task You Know Well

Sell one finished business task from your past job, done faster with AI

What It Is

You break your old job title into the individual tasks people always came to you for (scheduling, chasing information, writing emails, handling upset customers) and pick one that a small business would pay an outsider to finish every month. You then write an "operator document" of 10 rules and 10 examples of your best work to paste at the start of every AI chat, plus step-by-step instructions for the job itself. You price by the finished result, never by the hour, and aim for two clients at $500, one at $1,000 or four at $250. Clients come from telling 10 people you already know: former coworkers, businesses you buy from, relatives with small companies.

The Pitch

The creator says nobody pays for "AI"; they pay for a finished result, and AI just lets you deliver it in hours instead of weeks. She argues you should not abandon 10-20 years of experience to start over as an "AI consultant," and that businesses still pay the old human-labor price for these tasks, so the gap between price and your delivery cost is your profit. She says the first client may take 8 hours, the second 90 minutes and the tenth about 40 minutes once your steps are written down.

Who It's Best For

People with years of work experience in an office, admin or customer-facing role who are comfortable asking people they know for paid work.

The Truth (as we see it)

The core advice (sell an outcome you already know how to deliver, write clear rules for the AI, price per result) is sensible and widely repeated. The time savings and $500-$1,000 price points are the creator's own estimates, and she admits nobody is guaranteed $1,000 and that the price gap will close as businesses catch on. Finding clients through your personal network means real sales conversations, which is the step most people stall on. AI output still needs your review for accuracy before a client sees it. The creator pushes a free Sunday workshop (name and email required) and puts her prompts and outreach scripts behind description links; the video does not say what, if anything, is sold after the workshop. Her credentials ($10M in sales, Forbes feature) are self-reported.
