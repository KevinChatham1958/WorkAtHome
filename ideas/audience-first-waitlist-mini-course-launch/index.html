Audience-Led Mini-Course Launch

Audience-first mini-course: let followers' questions pick the product, then launch to a waitlist

What It Is

You post regularly on social media about something you know, watch which questions keep coming up, and answer them with a content series. When followers start asking for a paid resource, you open an interest list before building anything, and only create the course once hundreds sign up. You then open sales for a short window to the waitlist. The video breaks down an Instagram creator whose $57 video-editing mini-course came out of follower questions; she later relaunched it at a higher price.

The Pitch

The creator says the course brought in over $300,000 in six days and over $700,000 after a relaunch, and that it worked because the audience asked for it rather than the creator guessing. He argues the same audience-first, waitlist-first order works in any niche and avoids building products nobody wants.

Who It’s Best For

People willing to create content consistently for months and teach what they know, who are comfortable showing up on camera.

The Truth (as we see it)

The advice to confirm demand with a waitlist before building is sound and widely used. The revenue figures come from the featured creator's own screenshots and are unaudited, and she had spent about 10 years on Instagram and had 50,000 to 200,000 followers, so this is not a quick start. The featured creator says herself she has done it successfully only once, and outcomes like this are rare. The video's reach-based approach depends on growing an audience, which takes time and video-making skill.
