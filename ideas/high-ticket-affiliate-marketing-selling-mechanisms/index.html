High-Ticket Affiliate Funnels

High-ticket digital product affiliate marketing via webinar, book-a-call, or nurture funnels

Marketing & content · Very low startup cost (mainly time; an optional ad budget for paid traffic)

Originally published Aug 27, 2026 — Aaron Chen

Share on Facebook Link copied
Source

Aaron Chen · How People Make $1,000+ Per Sale With High-Ticket Affiliate Marketing ↗

What It Is

Become an affiliate for other people's high-priced digital products ($1,000-$5,000+ courses, coaching, and software) rather than cheap physical goods, earning 50-70% commissions through networks like Digistore24, ClickBank, JVZoo, and WarriorPlus. Rather than sending a cold affiliate link straight to a sales page, route prospects through one of three 'selling mechanisms': a live webinar, a 'book-a-call' funnel where a hired closer works the prospect on a Zoom call, or a multi-day 'nurture sequence' of value-packed videos that builds trust before the pitch.

The Pitch

The argument is that commissions on cheap physical products (a $20 t-shirt, a $50 gadget) are too small to matter, while digital products carry near-limitless perceived value and high commission rates, so a handful of $1,000+ sales a month beats thousands of tiny ones. The 'selling mechanism' is framed as the real skill: no matter how good the product or the traffic, sending someone straight to a $2,000-$5,000 sales page without first building trust through a webinar, a live call, or a nurture sequence simply won't convert.

Best For

Someone comfortable creating consistent content or running ads, and willing to either host webinars, hire/become a phone closer, or build out a multi-video email/DM sequence, rather than just dropping a bare affiliate link.

The Truth

The presenter's claimed ~$3 million in lifetime affiliate sales is self-reported with no independent verification, and the video funnels toward his own free masterclass. The webinar and book-a-call mechanisms in particular require either real public-speaking/sales comfort or budget to hire a closer, which raises the actual effort and cost well above the 'just sell someone else's stuff' pitch.
