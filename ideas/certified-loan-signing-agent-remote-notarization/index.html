Loan Signing Agent

What It Is

Get certified specifically as a loan signing agent, not just a general notary public — a role accompanying home buyers through mortgage closing to ensure documents are signed and notarized correctly, requiring precision rather than prior real estate experience. Build direct relationships with title companies and loan officers in your area rather than relying solely on assignment platforms that take a cut of your pay. Pursue remote online notarization, which lets you serve clients across multiple states without leaving home, multiplying how many appointments you can handle in a day.

The Pitch

The presenter cites data from Glassdoor and the Loan Signing System organization showing certified loan signing agents earn on average over $100,000/year, with individual appointments paying $75-200 for one to two hours of work, versus a traditional stand-alone notary earning only a few thousand dollars a year due to per-signature price caps in most states; he's honest that top earners consistently handle 20-40 appointments a month, which takes real time to build.

Who It’s Best For

Someone detail-oriented and comfortable with precision paperwork, willing to build direct relationships with title companies rather than depending entirely on assignment platforms.

The Truth (as we see it)

The cited income figures come from named third-party sources (Glassdoor, Loan Signing System) rather than the presenter's own unverified claim, and remote online notarization as a growing 2026 opportunity is a real, current development. The presenter is upfront that not everyone reaches six-figure income and that building a full appointment volume takes real time.
