Reaction/commentary Content Monetized Through Facebook's Creator Payouts

What It Is

Once a Facebook page crosses roughly 4,000 followers, Meta pays creators directly for views on reels, photos, and even stories, unlike Instagram, which generally doesn't pay for organic reach. The specific content strategy discussed is to find already-viral content in a chosen niche (money-making and side-hustle content, in the cited example), play the original clip in the background using a green-screen-style overlay, and record yourself reacting to and adding your own commentary and ideas on top of it, rather than silently pointing at the video.

The Pitch

The strategy is attributed to a named creator (Chris Corner) reportedly earning around $35,000 a month using this exact method, cranking out five to six of these reaction videos a day across niches once a winning format is found. The presenters note real, checkable audience-behavior context for why this works: Facebook's audience skews older and consumes far more content than it creates, while a lot of Instagram content already auto-posts to a linked Facebook page and quietly picks up far more views there than on Instagram itself, an underused distribution gap.

Best For

Someone comfortable being on camera for quick reaction-style commentary and willing to post multiple times a day, since the model depends on volume and finding already-proven viral source material rather than one polished video at a time.

The Truth

This entry is unusually well-sourced on its own limits: one of the presenters shows their own actual, real Facebook monetization earnings live on screen, a modest $4.68 total for the year from a largely inactive page, a genuinely transparent contrast to the $35,000-a-month claim, which is attributed secondhand to another creator's video and not independently verified. The presenters also flag a real platform risk: reposting content and talking about exactly how much money a platform's payout program earns has reportedly gotten some creators' accounts restricted or banned, worth knowing before building a strategy entirely around one platform's creator-payment program.
