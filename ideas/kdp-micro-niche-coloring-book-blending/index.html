Blended-Theme KDP Coloring Books

Micro-niche KDP coloring books built by blending trending themes and low-competition categories

  • Digital products
  • Very low startup cost

Jeff (5 TINY but Profitable KDP Niches) · 5 TINY but Profitable KDP Niches I'd Start Selling Right Now ↗

What It Is

Target small, low-competition KDP coloring/activity book niches (measured by total search results, percentage of top-page listings that are already KDP books, and niche/demand/opportunity scores) rather than chasing high-volume categories, using specific tactics: templating a themed idea across multiple countries/cultures (e.g. "Mexican Treats" coloring book applied to other national cuisines), merging two individually-trending animal/character niches into one book (e.g. axolotl + capybara), targeting event-specific niches with naturally short shelf-life demand (wedding kids' activity books), and building out comprehensive evergreen reference niches (a coloring book covering all 63 U.S. National Parks).

The Pitch

The presenter walks through live Amazon search data for each niche (total competing listings, how many are KDP vs. traditionally published, and estimated monthly royalties pulled from sales-rank data) and argues the strategy isn't finding one huge niche but building a catalog of 10-50 small books that each generate modest, steady, evergreen sales, illustrating the AI-generated-artwork workflow directly by creating sample coloring pages live using a credit-based AI tool.

Who It’s Best For

Someone willing to do niche research (checking competition and demand signals before publishing) and build a catalog of many small books over time rather than betting on one bestseller.

The Truth (as we see it)

Revenue figures cited are estimates derived from Amazon's public bestseller rank via third-party tools, a commonly used but indirect estimation method, not confirmed sales data. The core catalog-of-small-niches strategy is a genuinely different approach from chasing one viral topic and is a sound way to reduce single-book risk, though it also means no individual book is likely to produce large income on its own — the model depends on volume across many titles.
