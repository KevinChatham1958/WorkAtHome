Canvas Art From Your Own Site

Selling higher-priced canvas art through your own website and direct buyer relationships

  • $100–$500 to start

What It Is

You sell your artwork as canvas pieces, which command higher prices than paper prints. The creator averaged about $100 per canvas with about half as profit, reaching the same roughly $22,500 total profit as her prints while selling far fewer pieces. For these higher-priced items she recommends selling from your own website rather than a crowded marketplace, so you can build trust and help buyers pick a piece for a specific spot in their home.

The Pitch

She says canvases are her favorite product because people pay more for them, so each sale is worth much more. A direct relationship with repeat buyers suits a higher-ticket item better than being 'a random person' on Etsy or Zazzle, in her view.

Who It’s Best For

Artists with a distinct style or niche who are willing to build their own website and audience and can absorb occasional shipping losses.

The Truth (as we see it)

The pricing logic is sound, and her numbers are specific, though her own and unaudited. The risks she names are real: large canvases are expensive to ship, damage and returns happen, and you may eat the loss on roughly one in 20 or 30 sales, so prices must allow for that. Getting traffic to your own website is harder than listing on a marketplace and takes ongoing marketing. She warns against taking on many custom orders, which can become a time sink. No paid product is pitched.

Our assessment, based on the video and what’s typical across similar videos. We don’t independently verify income claims or prices.
