Coaching From Your Experience

"Sell before you teach" content strategy for building a coaching business around decades of professional experience

Content & personal brand · Very low startup cost (a phone to record content; no equipment or audience required to start)

Originally published Jul 29, 2026 — Steven Thompson

Share on Facebook Link copied
Source

Steven Thompson · Don't Teach on YouTube - Do THIS Instead to make your first $100K ↗

What It Is

Build a coaching or consulting business around decades of existing professional experience by selling directly to a small number of clients before creating any public content at all, then use content afterward not to teach complete how-to tutorials for free, but to share stories and insights that shift how an audience thinks about a problem — reserving the actual step-by-step implementation for a paid product — while targeting a specific type of person (e.g., people over 40 with an established career) rather than a narrow topic niche.

The Pitch

The presenter, a former police detective, cites a client named Rose, a travel consultant in her early 60s, who signed 6 clients at $2,500 each ($15,000 in her first month) before ever posting a single video, using her decades of niche expertise directly as the product. He also shares his own numbers: videos averaging around 400 views generated a $24,000 course launch, arguing that a small, already-convinced audience converts better than a large, undecided one, and that stopping full free tutorials (in favor of story-driven, belief-shifting content) grew his business more than teaching everything for free ever did.

Who It's Best For

Someone with substantial real-world professional experience (particularly people mid-career or later) who's willing to sell directly to a small initial audience via outreach rather than waiting to build a following first.

The Truth (as we see it)

The core reframes here — that experience itself can be the product regardless of content or audience size, and that giving away complete tutorials can undercut sales by resolving the very problem someone would pay to have solved — are genuinely useful correctives to common "just give away free value" content advice. The specific numbers describe strong individual examples (Rose's zero-video $15k month, the presenter's own 400-view/$24k launch) rather than typical outcomes, and the video is built toward promoting the presenter's own paid "YouTube business action plan" product.
