Faceless Channel Blueprint Videos

Faceless animated YouTube channel built from a Claude channel-analysis prompt and bulk AI visuals

Content & personal brand · Near-zero cost (free tiers of Claude, Google Flow, ElevenLabs and CapCut)

Originally published Sep 7, 2026 — Nuli Studio

Share on Facebook Link copied

Nuli Studio · How to Clone ANY YouTube Channel & Make 100s of Videos for FREE ↗

What It Is

You give Claude (free version) a long master prompt, a successful channel's URL, two or three of its transcripts and screenshots of its scenes; it works out the niche, hook style, script structure and visual style, suggests ten topics and writes a script of about 1,700 words split into roughly 30 scene beats with image prompts. All the prompts are pasted at once into Google Flow's AI agent, which generates every image in one go and can animate selected images into short clips using credits. A voiceover is made in ElevenLabs text-to-speech and everything is assembled in CapCut. The example is a monkey-cartoon channel explaining money concepts, and income would come from YouTube ad revenue once the channel qualifies for monetization.

The Pitch

The creator says bulk generation removes the slowest part of faceless video work, making scenes one by one, and that the whole workflow runs on free plans, shown with Google Flow's 50 free credits plus daily login credits. They say letting Claude reverse-engineer a proven channel's topics, storytelling and visuals gives you a tested blueprint instead of guessing, and point to a channel with 500,000 subscribers from 28 videos that VidIQ estimates earns over $3,000 a month.

Who It's Best For

Someone comfortable with AI tools and basic video editing who wants to run a faceless YouTube channel and is willing to publish for months before it can earn.

The Truth (as we see it)

The $3,000-a-month figure for the example channel is a third-party VidIQ estimate, not the creator's own earnings, and no results from channels built this way are shown. The workflow itself is clearly demonstrated and does run on free tiers, though video animation uses up free credits quickly. Copying another channel's style, characters and format closely risks being treated as reused or mass-produced content, which YouTube can refuse to monetize, so the output needs real originality to earn. The creator's master prompt is offered through their own channel, and the video does not say whether it is free.
