Wholesale 3D-Printed Toys

Wholesale 3D-printed toy company using printers you already own

E-commerce · Requires owning 3D printer(s); per-unit material/machine cost around $1.25 in the presenter's example

Originally published Aug 23, 2026 — How Much (Idan)

Share on Facebook Link copied
Source

How Much (Idan) · I Turned 3D Printers Into a Money Factory ↗

What It Is

Starting a wholesale 3D-printed toy company using 3D printers you already own, researching a popular toy design, setting up a Shopify store, and either holding light inventory or printing to order.

The Pitch

The presenter got the idea after noticing unlicensed 3D-printed toys being sold at a park and realizing there's already a market willing to pay for printed novelty items. He walks through the full same-day setup live: naming and buying a domain with Claude's help, setting up Shopify and Shopify Payments, calculating true per-unit cost including material, machine depreciation, and electricity, printing a sample at home, and using AI to generate consistent product photos in multiple colors from a single photograph.

Best For

Someone who already owns one or more 3D printers, or is willing to buy one, and wants to turn otherwise idle machine time into inventory, comfortable with print-on-demand-style fulfillment timelines.

The Truth

The presenter is upfront this is the first video in a planned series and doesn't yet show any actual sales results, just the setup process, so there's no revenue data to evaluate yet. He also flags a real legal consideration worth taking seriously: trademarked character designs are common in this space and need to be avoided, a caution that applies to anyone considering this idea.
