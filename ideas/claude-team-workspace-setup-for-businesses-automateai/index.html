What It Is

Many businesses already pay for Claude but staff use it as a random question box. You configure it around the company's recurring work: load their brand voice and processes, add instructions and examples, and set up starting points for tasks such as quotes and follow-up emails. You charge an initial setup fee, then offer an ongoing fee to keep the setup updated as the business changes and as Claude adds features. She suggests practicing first on three repetitive tasks at your current job and measuring the hours saved, to build a real example to show prospects.

The Pitch

The creator rates this highly because the company is already paying for the tool, which makes the first conversation easier. She says the offer gets much easier to sell when you tie the hours saved to money the business is already losing. Her own first build was a reporting fix for her employer's software, which later gave her a concrete story for client conversations.

Who It’s Best For

Organized people who understand how a particular kind of business runs day to day and are comfortable meeting owners to learn their workflows.

The Truth (as we see it)

This is a credible, practical service, since many teams have AI subscriptions they barely use, though the video gives no pricing for it and the barrier to entry is low, so competition will grow. Clients may struggle to see the value of saved time unless you measure it, as she herself notes. Expect client meetings and selling, and the setup must be maintained as the tools change. She promotes her own Skool community for learning to build and sell AI solutions, which is likely paid; her six-figure income claims are her own and unverified.

Our assessment, based on the video and what’s typical across similar videos. We don’t independently verify income claims or prices.
