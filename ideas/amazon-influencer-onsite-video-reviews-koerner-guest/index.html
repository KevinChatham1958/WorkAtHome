Amazon Influencer Video Reviews

Amazon Influencer video reviews that earn commission on product pages

Startup cost: Near-zero cost (a phone; you can review products you already own) (our estimate; the video doesn’t give figures)

Source video

Chris Koerner · The $0 Side Hustle Anyone Can Start ↗

What It Is

You film short phone videos reviewing products sold on Amazon and upload them through the Amazon Influencer Program, where they can appear in the video carousel on the product page. When a shopper watches your video and buys, you earn a commission. Koerner's guest explains the steps: get accepted to the program, then pass a second human review of your first three videos to get on-site placement, then pick products where your video can win one of the six carousel spots. You don't have to buy on Amazon: anything you own that's sold there counts, and established reviewers get brands to send free products.

The Pitch

The guest says people of all ages earn from this part-time, with examples from $10,000 to $40,000 a month, and that there's no inventory or overhead. Competition is per product rather than platform-wide, so a decent video on the right listing can keep earning for years; he cites one video still making about $150 a day two and a half years later.

Who It’s Best For

Anyone comfortable talking about products on camera, or showing them with their hands and a voiceover, who can produce videos steadily.

The Truth (as we see it)

The Amazon Influencer Program is real, and the approval steps described are accurate as far as we know. Commission rates are low (often a few percent, higher on some promoted items), so meaningful income takes hundreds or thousands of videos, and the top earners quoted are the guest's own picks. Amazon changes placement and commission rules without warning. The guest runs a paid tool for this program that finds products and automates outreach, so he has a stake in it looking easy. The video also touches on buying, reviewing and returning products, which they rightly call unethical.

Our assessment, based on the video and what’s typical across similar videos. We don’t independently verify income claims or prices.
