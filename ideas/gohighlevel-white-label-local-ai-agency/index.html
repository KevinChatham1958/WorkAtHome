White-Label Marketing Agency

White-label marketing agency for established local businesses, run entirely inside GoHighLevel

What It Is

You run a one-person marketing agency for local businesses (realtors, electricians, med spas, tax firms) from a single platform, GoHighLevel, and white-label it as your own service. An AI prospecting agent finds leads (his demo: 12 electrician leads a week in Houston, run every Monday). Each client gets a sub-account where you build AI-generated funnels and websites, A/B test funnels, run Google review and reputation campaigns, set up appointment-reminder text workflows, manage social media and email, and even host courses or communities.

The Pitch

Enfroy pitches this as a faster path to money than content because clients pay monthly instead of you waiting on affiliate commissions or course sales. He says to target 'small' businesses that are actually multi-million-dollar operations with 30-40 employees or several locations. Those can pay $4,000-$5,000 a month instead of hiring a $5,000-$6,000 marketing manager, and up to $10,000 a month if you serve a vertical like software companies. His math: five clients at $5,000 is $25,000 a month, minus about $10,000 for staff, leaves $15,000. He suggests doing the first client's work yourself and hiring a junior specialist by client two. He says one person can manage 5-10 clients with the automations.

Who It’s Best For

Someone with sales or marketing experience who's comfortable prospecting and talking to business owners, and wants recurring service income rather than building an audience first.

The Truth (as we see it)

This is essentially a product tour of GoHighLevel, promoted through his affiliate link, and it also plugs his paid 'Launch to 10K' program, which teaches the agency model. The tools shown are real, but the video skips the hard part: actually closing $4,000-$5,000-a-month clients, which takes sales skill and proof of results. The $15,000 profit figure is a hypothetical, not a result he shows. GoHighLevel's pricing isn't stated, so check costs before committing.
