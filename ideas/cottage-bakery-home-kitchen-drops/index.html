Home Cottage Bakery

Cottage bakery selling weekly product drops from a home kitchen

Physical & print products · Low startup cost (a food handler certificate plus basic baking ingredients and packaging)

What It Is

Bake a single signature product (Zaback's example: cinnamon rolls) from a home kitchen under your state's cottage food laws, take pre-orders online, and sell in scheduled weekly drops rather than running a storefront.

The Pitch

Zaback profiles Lindsey Skillnick (Mama Lama Bakery), who started with a driveway farm stand and moved to online pre-orders, now selling roughly 400 $12 cinnamon rolls most weekends (about $4,800/weekend, over $20,000/month) on ingredient costs of $5-7 per dozen. He frames the legal bar to entry as low in most states: a food handler certificate (about $10-15), state cottage-food registration, and proper labeling, with no commercial kitchen or storefront required.

Best For

Someone who already enjoys baking and wants to test a home-based food business without the overhead of a commercial kitchen lease, and who's comfortable managing a pre-order/drop schedule.

The Truth

Cottage food laws vary significantly by state — some cap annual revenue, restrict which products can be sold this way, or don't allow online sales at all — so the "just three things" legal summary in the video should be checked against your own state's specific cottage food program before starting. The revenue figures are one baker's self-reported numbers after building a following, not a starting point; going from zero to viral in under 90 days, as Skillnick reportedly did, is closer to the exception than the rule.
