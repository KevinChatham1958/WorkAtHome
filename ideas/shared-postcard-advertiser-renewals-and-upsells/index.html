Shared Postcard Advertiser Retention

Keeping advertisers renewing on a monthly shared community postcard and upselling them marketing services

  • $500–$2,000 to start

Video published Oct 30, 2026 by The 9x12 Method

The 9x12 Method · How 9x12ers Keep Advertisers Paying Card After Card ↗

What It Is

For people already running a 9x12 shared postcard (about 16 local businesses each paying around $500 for a slot on a card mailed to roughly 5,000 homes), this covers how to fill the second and later cards with the same advertisers. The creator's steps: offer to hold their spot on next month's card when sending the final proof, send short updates at each stage (approved, printing, at the post office, landing), call or text each advertiser about a week after the drop and ask whether anyone mentioned the card, ask for referrals, try to sell a whole side of the card to one anchor business, raise the price for new advertisers, plan around seasonal industries, and track everything in a CRM. Once trust is earned, the card leads to upsells such as logos, websites and Google profile work.

The Pitch

The creator says selling 16 strangers is the hard part and selling the same 16 people again is a different, easier job, because they already know you and saw their ad in the mailbox. She calls the card a "Trojan horse" for agency services and gives member examples, including one who sold eight spots to a single roofing company.

Who It’s Best For

People comfortable selling to local business owners by phone, email and social media, who want a repeat monthly product.

The Truth (as we see it)

The retention advice is practical and honest, including warnings not to upsell before the first card mails and not to promise services you can't deliver. Results depend on advertisers seeing a return from mail, which many small businesses can't measure, so churn is a real risk. The business relies on US postal mail routes and steady monthly selling. The creator sells a paid community with the course and templates, a printing service, and the 9x12 Method Accelerator one-on-one coaching program, which the video promotes.
