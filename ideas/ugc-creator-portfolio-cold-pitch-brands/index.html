UGC Creator for Brands

UGC creator making short product videos for brands, built on a household-product portfolio and cold email pitches

  • Under $100 to start

Melanie Renee · How to start UGC in 2027 💸 (I made $50K in my first year) ↗

What It Is

You film short videos of yourself using a product (hooks, a demo, a quick story and a call to action) that brands run in their own ads and social accounts, so you don't need followers. She says to spend a week studying UGC examples on TikTok and Instagram, then script, film and edit eight sample videos of products already in your home in different formats (talking head, green screen, split screen, product demo) and put them in a portfolio site built from a cheap Canva template. You land work by applying to campaigns on UGC platforms or by cold-emailing brands, finding contacts with a tool like Bento or Apollo and sending three follow-ups. She suggests a floor of about $100 per video for beginners, pitching higher and raising rates every ten or so deals, and charging extra for usage rights and extra hooks.

The Pitch

The creator says she made over $50,000 in her first year of UGC with no following and only a phone, a mic and a tripod, landing four-figure deals where she never had to post on her own page. She argues brands want everyday-looking people because shoppers trust them more than polished influencers. She adds that posting your UGC-style videos on your own social accounts brings in inbound brand requests, and that people of all ages, including retirees, are starting.

Who It’s Best For

People comfortable on camera who can script and edit short videos and are willing to pitch brands repeatedly before the first paid deals come in.

The Truth (as we see it)

UGC is a real and widely used service, and her advice on contracts (payment terms, usage rights, scope creep) and not undercharging is practical. The $50,000 figure is her own and unaudited, and she already had a growing social and YouTube presence, which she says brings her inbound deals that a beginner won't have. The field is crowded, rates for newcomers are low, and her own first $100 job took six to eight hours. She spends much of the video demonstrating Poppy AI, which she uses daily and links in her description; you don't need it to do this work.

Our assessment, based on the video and what’s typical across similar videos. We don’t independently verify income claims or prices.
