Knife and Tool Sharpening Route for Restaurants, Butchers, and Salons

What It Is

Sharpen knives, scissors, and other bladed tools for restaurants, butchers, and salons, structured as a swap route where you drop off a freshly sharpened duplicate set and pick up the dull set, turning a one-time job into a recurring weekly stop.

The Pitch

The presenter recommends skipping the commonly-suggested $1,066 Tormek T8 in favor of a $170-250 Work Sharp Ken Onion or a roughly $300 belt sander setup. Rates from operators running restaurant routes: $3.50/inch for blades under 4 inches, $4.50 for 4-7 inches, $6 over 7 inches, and around $4.50 for scissors, which one operator called the best margin on the route. A soft kitchen knife takes roughly 1-3 minutes on a belt grinder, while hand-sharpening with stones (about 45 minutes per blade) is explicitly called a hobby pace, not a route pace.

Best For

Someone willing to build a recurring weekly route with commercial accounts (restaurants, butchers, salons) using a duplicate-set swap system, rather than one-off sharpening jobs for individual consumers.

The Truth

The swap-route structure is the real insight here — it's what turns sharpening from a single transaction into recurring weekly revenue — and the presenter is specific about which equipment tier is actually needed versus commonly over-recommended gear. The main unaddressed challenge is landing the initial commercial accounts, which requires direct B2B sales outreach.
