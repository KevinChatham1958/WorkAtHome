Branded Apparel Dropshipping

Branded apparel dropshipping brand economics (monthly transparency breakdown)

E-commerce · Needs ongoing ad budget (six-figure monthly spend at Rabin's scale, though the store itself costs very little to set up)

Originally published Aug 19, 2026 — Austin Rabin

Share on Facebook Link copied
Source

Austin Rabin · $262k in 30 days with branded Shopify A.I. (FULL STORE REVEAL) ↗

What It Is

Run a branded apparel dropshipping store (Rabin's example: golf-branded polos) on Shopify, using seasonal collection drops, Facebook and Shopify's own "Shop" ad channel, and email/SMS marketing, with fulfillment handled by a linked supplier (Zendrop).

The Pitch

Rabin opens his own July numbers: $262,471 in revenue against $130,543 in Facebook ad spend, $62,004 in cost of goods, and other fees, netting $42,301 (about 16% profit margin) after a mid-month Facebook ad account issue cut into results. He credits a themed seasonal drop (a 4th of July "Americana" collection) and a bundle offer (5 polos for $250) for driving the month, and introduces Shopify's own "Shop" ad channel as a pay-per-sale alternative to Facebook ads.

Best For

Someone who already has, or is building toward, a real ad budget and wants a realistic look at what a mid-six-figure-revenue dropshipping brand's actual expenses and margins look like, rather than a revenue-only highlight reel.

The Truth (as we see it)

This is a genuinely detailed, numbers-forward breakdown, which is rarer and more useful than most dropshipping content. But the figures describe an already-scaled operation spending over $130,000/month on ads, not a starting point for someone with no budget — and the 16% margin shown is described as a below-average month for this brand. It's also a promotional video for the same free store-builder and Zendrop supplier tool featured in Rabin's other video in this batch.
