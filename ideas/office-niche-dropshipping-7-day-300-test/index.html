$300 Dropshipping Store Test

Office-gadget dropshipping store tested on a $300, seven-day budget

E-commerce · Low startup cost (about $300 here: ad spend, sample orders, discounted Shopify and AutoDS)

Originally published Nov 3, 2026 — Mark Tilbury

Share on Facebook Link copied
Source

Mark Tilbury · I Tried AI Dropshipping For 7 Days, Copy Every Step ↗

What It Is

An experienced dropshipper builds a store in a randomly drawn niche (office gadgets) with a $300 limit and seven days, filming every step. He picks products using a supplier marketplace's trending and engagement data, favoring items that solve a clear problem, can be explained in three seconds, cost under about $80, and can sell for two to three times the supplier price. He builds a Shopify store with an AI store builder, orders samples, films short promotional videos modeled on competitors' viral ads, imports supplier reviews, and runs Facebook ads. The final tally was 25 orders, $855.75 in revenue, $249.66 in ad spend and a $54.01 profit after product, shipping and fees.

The Pitch

The channel host frames it as the most detailed walkthrough he has published, arguing that sales were rising each day and a 30-day run would likely have done better.

Who It's Best For

Beginners who want to see a realistic, numbers-included first week of dropshipping and are prepared for thin early profits.

The Truth (as we see it)

The published result is the useful part: a ten-year expert, a full week of work and $250 of ads produced only about $54 profit, which is a fair picture of how slim early dropshipping margins are. The claim that a longer run would scale is untested in the video. The supplier app and store builder used appear to be partners, and the expert promotes his own paid "cheat sheet"; treat tool recommendations accordingly. Expect customer service, refunds and slow shipping to be part of the job.
