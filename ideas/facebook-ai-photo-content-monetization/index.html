Facebook Photo Page

Building a niche Facebook photo page monetized through Facebook's Content Monetization program

Content & personal brand · Very low (an AI image generation tool, free Facebook Page creation, optional basic photo editor)

Originally published Aug 14, 2026 — Passive Biz Lucca

Share on Facebook Link copied

Passive Biz Lucca · How You can Post Images on Facebook and Quit Your Job ↗

What It Is

Pick one narrow, highly visual niche (cozy cabins, vintage cars, tiny homes), validate demand by checking for existing large Facebook pages in that niche, create a public Facebook Page around it, generate original AI images with a specific, detail-rich prompt (not generic), lightly edit each image to make it feel human-made, post one per day with a short caption ending in a question to drive comments, and apply to Facebook's Content Monetization program once the page hits roughly 5,000-10,000 followers with real engagement.

The Pitch

The presenter reports over $14,000 in one month from Facebook content monetization (mostly PDF sales plus the photo program) and walks through the mechanic: Facebook now pays for original photos and text posts, not just video, but has cracked down hard on reposted or lightly-edited content since 2025, so the images must be genuinely original. He gives a specific 90-day, one-post-a-day plan with a weekly review step, and is upfront this is a slow-build strategy, not a fast-cash scheme.

Who It's Best For

Someone willing to commit to a consistent 90-day daily-posting habit in one visual niche, comfortable writing detailed AI image prompts and doing basic photo-editing polish, patient with a slow initial ramp.

The Truth (as we see it)

Facebook's content monetization program and its original-content enforcement rules are real and match public information from Facebook's creator resources, and the described mechanics are consistent with how the platform's recommendation system is known to work. The specific $14,000/month figure is self-reported and unverifiable, payout thresholds and eligibility vary by country and change over time (the video itself warns not to trust fixed numbers), and it ends by funneling viewers to a paid course for the full system.
