Faceless screen-recorded mini courses sold on Etsy (Claude-scripted)

What It Is

You make a short course of five to ten brief video lessons, or even a single 20-minute tutorial plus a PDF, teaching one specific skill, and sell it as a digital download on Etsy or other platforms. The creator uses Claude to plan six to eight lessons with a one-line promise each and to write lesson scripts in plain spoken language. You record your screen with a voiceover, so your face never appears, and AI voice tools are an option if you prefer not to use your own voice. Related courses can be bundled into a higher-priced listing.

The Pitch

The creator says you only need to be one step ahead of the buyer, not an expert. His example is a chunky knit blanket tutorial selling for about $15.59 that an estimate tool shows earning close to $400 a month. He says this is the product you can price highest of the four, and that you are selling a result rather than just a video.

Who It's Best For

People with a practical skill (a craft, a software setup, a work system) who can explain it clearly on a voiceover.

The Truth (as we see it)

Short how-to courses can sell when they teach a concrete, searchable outcome, as the knitting example does. The creator admits this is a smaller category on Etsy than templates, and the one example he shows earns a modest amount. Your own real knowledge matters more than the AI script; a course written mostly by AI on a topic you do not know well is likely to disappoint buyers. The creator says Claude builds it in minutes, but buyers on Etsy compare many near-identical AI-built listings, so niche choice and presentation matter more than build speed. The creator sells a paid program (a 20-product-type module plus traffic training), gives away the prompts in exchange for an email address, and repeatedly promotes the Everbee research tool through a referral link.
