AI Google Review Replies

AI-drafted Google review replies with an owner voice-guide and approval dashboard

What It Is

Build a "voice guide" in Claude from a business's existing reviews and how the owner naturally talks, then have the system read each new incoming review, pull the specific detail the customer mentioned (a staff name, the exact service performed), and draft a personalized reply in the owner's voice — critically, no reply publishes automatically; each draft lands on a dashboard where the owner reviews and clicks approve before it goes live on Google, which is what makes owners comfortable saying yes and avoids platform risk.

The Pitch

The sales hook leans on a cited 2026 survey finding that the share of people using AI assistants for local business recommendations jumped from 6% to 45% in a year, meaning unanswered reviews are increasingly the raw material AI tools cite when recommending (or not recommending) a business — while the same survey found generic templated replies ("thanks for your feedback" 50 times) actively turn off half of customers, which is the specific gap this service closes. A strong pre-call tactic: have Claude read a prospect's full review history and hand them a one-page summary of their top 5 complaints and top 5 praises before ever pitching anything, which most owners have never seen. Priced $500-1,500/month based on location count and review volume.

Who It’s Best For

Someone targeting multi-location or review-heavy local businesses, comfortable positioning the pre-call complaint/praise summary as a free diagnostic to open conversations.

The Truth (as we see it)

A similar AI-drafted review-reply concept already exists in this database from a different producer at a flat $199/month; this version's distinguishing specifics are the owner-approval-before-publish workflow (a meaningful trust and platform-compliance safeguard) and the pre-call complaint/praise diagnostic as a sales tool. The cited survey statistic (6% to 45% AI-assistant usage) should be verified against the original Bright Local source rather than taken at face value, as exact figures from cited surveys are sometimes rounded or summarized loosely in marketing content.
