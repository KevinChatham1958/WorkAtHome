Facebook Commentary Payouts

Earning Facebook creator payouts by adding your own commentary to viral content, then capturing emails

  • YouTube & blogging
  • Under $100 to start
  • Jump to The Truth ↓

Video published Jan 27, 2026 by Chris Koerner

Startup cost: Very low startup cost (a phone, an optional clip-on mic, free editing apps like CapCut or Edits)

Source video

Chris Koerner · How I Make $35k/Month With Other People's Content (Legally) ↗

What It Is

Use a fresh Instagram account trained on your niche to find reels, tweets or images that are already viral, then make your own version that adds real insight: a green-screen video of you giving your take over the original clip, or a screenshot with your own opinion added. Upload natively to Facebook and to each other platform separately (never using the cross-post toggle), tag the original creator where possible, and wait for Facebook to invite you into its monetization program. Put a link to a simple email signup page (Carrd or beehiiv) in the top comment of every post, and earn extra through a referral network like SparkLoop, which pays per newsletter signup.

The Pitch

Koerner says he earns $10,000-$35,000 a month in profit from Facebook in about three hours a week, with single posts earning $70 to $2,000, around 90% of it repurposed content with his commentary added, on accounts he started from zero less than two years ago. He says Facebook pays because it has over two billion daily users but too few people posting, cites about $0.25 per thousand views on short videos, says SparkLoop pays $0.50-$5 per email, and credits Facebook traffic for most of his newsletter's growth from 96,000 to 250,000 subscribers.

Who It’s Best For

Someone with opinions or expertise in a niche who can record quick selfie videos consistently and is willing to wait months for monetization.

The Truth (as we see it)

The legal line matters here: genuine commentary can qualify as fair use, but plain reaction videos or reposts can be flagged or get an account banned, and fair use is decided case by case, not guaranteed. Koerner himself warns against plain reaction videos. Facebook's monetization is by invitation with no published threshold, so timing is uncertain, and he notes a new account reaches only a few hundred people at first. His income comes from an established creator with a large audience elsewhere, the video includes a paid beehiiv sponsorship, and a beginner's results will likely be much smaller.
