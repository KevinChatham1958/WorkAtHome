Micro-Niche AI Coloring Books

Micro-niche AI coloring books made with Claude and Higgsfield, sold on Amazon KDP and Etsy

Physical & print products · Low startup cost (Claude and Higgsfield or similar image-tool subscriptions)

What It Is

You connect Claude to an image generator (Higgsfield) through an MCP connector so Claude can plan a themed book and generate the pages itself. Claude picks a micro-niche (a dog breed, zodiac signs, cottagecore, a hobby), lists about 30 page concepts, and writes coloring-specific prompts: clean black outlines, white background, no shading, bold lines. You export the pages at 300 DPI at 8.5 x 11 inches, color one page in for the cover, and publish the same artwork twice: as a print-on-demand paperback on Amazon KDP (typically $7-$13, up to $15 for bold-and-easy books) and as a PDF download on Etsy ($1.50-$3 per page, $7-$20 per book). Marketing is free: Pinterest pins of single pages, TikTok flip-through videos, long-tail listing keywords and a free sample page.

The Pitch

The creator says one book can earn "between 50 and 500 dollars a month" and that micro-niches often have under 500 direct competitors, unlike the 100,000+ generic titles on Amazon. He points to bold-and-easy books for seniors and beginners, and grayscale photo coloring, as current trends. He argues AI removes the old barrier of needing to draw, and that selling one file on both KDP and Etsy multiplies the return on the same work.

Best For

Patient people who enjoy niche research and steady catalog building, and who want low-cost, no-inventory products they can make entirely at home.

The Truth

The workflow is practical and the creator gives useful honest cautions: Amazon requires you to disclose AI-generated images at upload, is cracking down on low-quality high-volume publishing, and rejects copyrighted characters, trademarks and real people. The $50-$500 per book figure is the creator's own estimate, and the video does not show any actual sales despite the title; many low-content books sell little or nothing, so income depends on niche choice and building a catalog over time. The creator says he is not selling a course and none is pitched, but the method needs paid Claude and image-tool subscriptions. KDP royalties on low-priced paperbacks are small after printing costs, so margins per sale are thin.
