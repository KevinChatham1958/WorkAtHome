Starting a YouTube Channel Built on Your Own Life and Work Experience (aimed at People Over 40) That Attracts Buyers for Your Coaching or Services

Content & personal brand · Low (a camera or phone, free tools like a vidIQ free account)

Originally published Sep 27, 2026 — Steven Thompson

Share on Facebook Link copied
Source

Steven Thompson · How To Make YouTube Videos Everyone Loves To Watch - Over 40, Do This! ↗

What It Is

Use decades of work and life experience to build a YouTube channel that brings in paying clients for your coaching, services or programs, rather than relying on ad revenue. Pick video topics from the questions people already ask you, check with a keyword tool (e.g. vidIQ) that people search for them, and aim content at the person you were 5-10 years ago who needed that help. The presenter teaches a repeatable five-part structure for each video: a hook that delivers on the title's promise and names the viewer's main problems; a short 'mindset' question to connect with the viewer; a personal story; one single teaching point; and a clear next-step call to action. He encourages 'ugly', one-take videos to get started rather than waiting for perfection.

The Pitch

The presenter says he has run a social media agency for over 13 years and that clients using this system inside his paid program make around $25,000 a month from buyers, not AdSense. He argues that people over 40 have experience younger creators can't copy, and that answering real questions in smaller search niches is easier than competing with big channels.

Best For

People over 40 with deep experience in a field who want to turn it into a coaching or service business and are willing to show up on camera regularly, even imperfectly.

The Truth

The video-structure advice (strong hook, one idea per video, a clear call to action, niching down to questions people actually search) is standard, sensible YouTube practice. The $25,000/month client figure is an unverified claim used to promote the presenter's own paid program and community, and the video gives no evidence for it. The video is mostly about how to make content; the business side (what to sell, pricing, how viewers turn into clients) isn't covered, so a viewer would need to work that out separately. Building a YouTube audience large enough to produce steady clients usually takes many months of consistent posting.
