Renting Lead-Gen Microsites

Renting ranked lead-gen microsites to contractors (per-lead, revenue share, bundle or prime-contractor deals)

What It Is

You build small exact-match-domain websites for one high-ticket, rarely repeated service in one town (examples given: tree removal, roofing, foundation repair, water damage, sewer line repair), put a tracked phone number on each, and rank them on Google. Once calls come in, you rent the lead flow to a local contractor rather than doing the work yourself. The video lays out four ways to charge: per lead ($100-150), a 5-10% revenue share of each closed job (an 8% cut of a $9,000 roof is $720), a flat monthly fee for a bundle of sites (around $1,000-2,000 a month), or acting as a 'prime contractor' who qualifies and closes the customer and hands the job to a tradesperson. He suggests opening with a free first week of calls, then per-lead billing, then moving the contractor to flat rent.

The Pitch

The creator compares it to owning a rental property 'without the tenants trashing it': you own the domains, rankings and phone numbers, so the contractor can't cut you out the way an SEO client can fire an agency. He says one closed job can pay the contractor's whole monthly rent, which makes the pitch easy, and that rent can rise as rankings and call volume grow. He targets 'hungry operators' (contractors running ads or stuck on page two) because they have fewer alternatives and tend to stay.

Who It’s Best For

Someone comfortable with basic website building and local SEO who is also willing to phone and negotiate with local tradespeople. It suits people who want to build a portfolio of small assets over months rather than earn quickly.

The Truth (as we see it)

Rank-and-rent is a long-established lead-generation model, and the pricing structures described (per lead, revenue share, flat retainer) are standard in the lead-gen trade. The hard parts are the ones the creator himself flags: getting sites to rank, finding a contractor who will pay, and agreeing what counts as a 'qualified' lead; revenue-share deals depend on trusting the contractor to report closed jobs honestly. Community income examples and the claim that ranking is 'easy mode' are the creator's own and unaudited, and results depend heavily on niche and town choice. The 'prime contractor' option involves meeting customers in person at job sites, so it is not fully home-based. Jesse Cunningham sells membership in his own Rank Expand Academy (a paid online community with a 7-day free trial, Microsite Playbook and Microsite Empire modules, weekly calls and a one-click microsite builder) and promotes it throughout the video, along with his niche-scoring tool at tool.rankexpand.com.
