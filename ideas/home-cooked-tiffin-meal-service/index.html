Home-Cooked Meal Service

Home-cooked tiffin meal service for busy professionals

Physical & print products · Low startup cost (containers, ingredients; local food-business permit where required)

What It Is

Cook simple daily meals in your own kitchen and sell them by subscription to busy working people who don't have time to cook, packed in basic containers and handed off or delivered nearby. The video's math is 20 to 25 meals a day at about $3 to $4 profit each, which it puts at roughly $1,800 to $3,000 a month. Overhead stays low because there is no storefront, only ingredients and packaging.

The Pitch

The video argues that food is a need that never goes away in good or bad economies, and a home kitchen keeps costs low enough to start alongside a job.

Who It's Best For

Good home cooks who can produce the same meals reliably every day and live where there are enough office workers or busy households nearby.

The Truth (as we see it)

Home meal businesses are real and common, especially in South Asia where the tiffin model is established, but the profit figures are the video's own illustration, not a measured result. Many places restrict selling food made in a home kitchen without a permit or cottage-food license, and some ban perishable meals entirely, so check local rules before taking orders. Daily cooking for 20+ people is steady physical work with little room for days off. The video is a general faceless explainer listing nine businesses, so treat it as a prompt to research rather than a how-to.
