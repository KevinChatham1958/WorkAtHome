Thrifted Clothing on eBay

Sourcing clothes from flea markets, thrift stores, and bulk clothing pallets to resell on eBay

  • Online stores
  • Very low to start

What It Is

Source clothing from flea markets, thrift stores, personal/family closets, and eventually bulk clothing pallets or full truckloads (sold cheap because ~90 million pounds of clothing gets discarded daily in the US), clean and photograph each item (12 photos per item, keyword-rich titles like 'parka military jacket' for eBay search visibility), and list following an 80/20 rule: 80% everyday mid-grade items ($20-80) that sell predictably, with a small 20% (or less) of higher-end pieces ($100-600+) as pure-profit upside; track inventory with a simple sequential numbering system (001 to infinity, never repeating) to manage high volume without a fancy racking system.

The Pitch

The interviewed reseller reports $120,000 in revenue last month at a 50-60% profit margin, having grown from an $18,000/month one-person thrift/flea-market operation to now moving 9,000 items/month and buying 44,000-lb bulk truckloads, with a stated goal of $6 million next year including wholesale; he's candid that losing his prior electronics supplier nearly ended the business a year ago, crediting diversifying across many sourcing channels rather than depending on one as the key lesson.

Who It’s Best For

Someone willing to start small (even just their own closet) and do hands-on sourcing, cleaning, photographing, and shipping, comfortable scaling into bulk buying and eventually hiring help as volume grows.

The Truth (as we see it)

Clothing reselling via thrift/flea-market sourcing and bulk liquidation pallets is a well-established, real e-commerce niche, and the specific tactics described (keyword-rich titles, the 80/20 grade mix, sequential inventory numbering, multi-supplier diversification) reflect genuine operational knowledge. His revenue and margin figures are self-reported and represent 8 years of compounding growth, not a typical first-year result, and the video includes a paid sponsorship segment (a business-formation service) that is promotional rather than neutral advice.
