Pre-Sold Expertise Ebook

Writing an expertise-based ebook pre-sold to an email list grown from weekly YouTube videos

Digital & subscription products · Near-zero cost (free AI, Canva and Google Forms; a checkout page)

Originally published Oct 12, 2026 — Molly Keyser

Share on Facebook Link copied
Source

Molly Keyser · Selling EBooks with Claude Changed My Life (DON'T Sell on Etsy) ↗

What It Is

You pick a problem you have solved for yourself or others, post a weekly educational YouTube video on it, and offer a short free guide in exchange for an email address. At about 200 subscribers you survey them with Google Forms to find their biggest pain point, then put up a checkout page describing the ebook, its price and a delivery date a few weeks out, and email the list to pre-sell it. Only if people buy do you write it, using Claude trained on your own past emails, captions or dictated notes (via a voice-to-text tool) and laying it out in Canva. The creator's example was a 13-page $59 ebook teaching photographers her method for booking clients.

The Pitch

The creator says her $59 ebook made over $506,000 over the years it was on sale, with no ads, no sales calls and no large following at the start, and that she later sold its rights to another educator. She says pre-selling stops you wasting months on something nobody wants, a lesson she learned when her first paid guide, built on a guess, flopped. Her point is that what sells is your own experience and way of teaching, not generic information an AI could produce in seconds.

Who It's Best For

Someone with real know-how in a specific field who is willing to appear on YouTube weekly and build an email list before expecting sales.

The Truth (as we see it)

The $506,000 figure and the student result are the creator's own claims and unaudited, and her ebook sold into an audience of photographers who already knew her as a top-earning studio owner, which a beginner will not have. The method itself, survey your list then pre-sell before building, is sound and low-risk. The video repeatedly promotes the creator's free training, which leads into her paid digital-products coaching, and includes a referral link for the voice-to-text tool. Expect months of weekly videos before an email list is large enough to sell to.
