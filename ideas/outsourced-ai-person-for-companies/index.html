What It Is

You become the go-to AI expert for businesses that want to use AI but have nobody on staff who keeps up with it. You learn the tools, share what you're learning publicly, and get hired on a contract or retainer to set up and run AI work for a company.

The Pitch

Dang's example is his client Shawn, who he says had a technical background but had only been studying AI for under six months. Shawn posted AI content on YouTube and LinkedIn, picked up high-ticket clients from it, and then landed a contract Dang describes as worth six figures to be the 'AI guy' for a large media company. Dang's point is that busy executives will pay someone who has put in the hours of learning so they don't have to.

Who It’s Best For

People with some technical aptitude who enjoy learning new tools constantly and can explain them clearly to non-technical managers.

The Truth (as we see it)

There is real demand for people who can put AI to practical use inside companies, and publishing what you learn is a sensible way to get noticed. The six-figure contract is one client story, described without names or details, and is not verifiable. Shawn's technical background likely mattered more than the story suggests. Keeping up with fast-changing AI tools is ongoing work. The video carries a Higgsfield sponsorship and promotes Dang's free Claude skill, live training and coaching.
