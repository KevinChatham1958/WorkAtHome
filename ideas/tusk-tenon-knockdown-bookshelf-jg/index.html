Knock-Down Tusk Tenon Bookshelf

Building a no-hardware, take-apart leaning bookshelf held together by wedged tusk tenon joints

  • $100–$500 to start

Startup cost: Low to moderate startup cost (jigsaw, Forstner bits, chisels and mallet, table saw for trimming, stain and polyurethane; 1x12 pine boards) (our estimate; the video doesn’t give figures)

What It Is

You build a four-shelf leaning bookshelf from 1x12 pine in a home workshop and sell it as a piece that needs no screws, nails or glue. The two 40-inch sides are cut at 5 degrees, each shelf gets 3-by-3-inch corners cut away to leave a tab (tenon), and the tabs pass through slots in the sides and lock in place with wedge-shaped wooden keys. The creator drills out the slots and key holes with a 3/4-inch Forstner bit, squares them with a chisel, fills knots with black CA glue, stains it dark walnut and uses a water-based polyurethane that cures in about three days so the parts don't stick together. He shares a free cut sheet in the video.

The Pitch

The creator calls this a best-selling piece of furniture and thinks the visible wedged joints are what sell it. Because it comes apart with no hardware, it can be shipped flat and the buyer can put it together without tools.

Who It’s Best For

An intermediate woodworker who is comfortable with chisels and angled cuts, or someone with a CNC machine who could cut the parts in batches.

The Truth (as we see it)

The creator's own verdict is that this is not worth it for him: it took him many hours, he made several costly mistakes with the 5-degree angles and blown-out holes, and he says he would make little or no money building it by hand. He thinks it only makes sense if you have a CNC machine to batch the parts. He calls it a best seller but shows no listings, prices or sales numbers, and construction-grade 1x12s vary in width and warp, so expect waste and refitting. Shipping a 40-inch shelf, even flat, will be costly.
