AI Dropshipping Ad Scaling

AI dropshipping store scaled with AI-recreated competitor ads and an advertorial pre-lander page

  • Online stores
  • $500–$2,000 to start
  • Jump to The Truth ↓

Video published Nov 13, 2026 by MoMike Chamberlin

Startup cost: Needs ongoing ad budget (about $50 to $575 a day in this test, plus Shopify, AutoDS and AI tool subscriptions)

Source video

MoMike Chamberlin · I Tried AI Dropshipping For 14 Days ($10,000+) ↗

What It Is

You run a Shopify dropshipping store, here a children's art-storage frame, with AutoDS placing supplier orders, tracking stock and price, and sending tracking numbers automatically (3 to 5 day shipping instead of 3 to 5 weeks from random AliExpress sellers). For ads, you take a top-performing competitor video from TikTok Shop and have Claude, connected to the Higgsfield AI tool, recreate it with a new AI character, then stitch clips into ads and spend about an hour a day making more. On Meta you run a main campaign plus a test campaign for ads that weren't getting spend, and later a campaign sending traffic to a blog-style "pre-lander" page that lists reasons parents buy the product. In his figures, day 14 brought about $1,800 in sales and $482 profit after $576 in ads and $760 in product costs, and total sales reached about $21,800 by day 21.

The Pitch

The creator says a steady flow of new ads is what fed Meta's optimisation and turned a losing store profitable, and that the pre-lander campaign hit about a 4x return on ad spend. He argues few people are using AI for e-commerce yet, so it is early.

Who It’s Best For

People who can handle several days of losses while testing and who will make new ads every day.

The Truth (as we see it)

The day-by-day profit and loss figures, including losing days, are more candid than most dropshipping videos, but they are the creator's own and unaudited, and the store was run with an experienced partner. Recreating a competitor's best ad "and make it my own" can cross into copying, and blog-style pre-landers and AI "mom" characters can mislead buyers if not clearly presented as ads. The video is sponsored by AutoDS (Build Your Store), and the creators say they are making a mini course on the method.
