Recovered Golf Ball Resale

Recovered golf ball resale (clean, grade and ship from home)

E-commerce · Low to moderate startup cost (about $500 for first batch, scale, bins, cleaning and shipping supplies)

Originally published Oct 1, 2026 — Austin Zaback

Share on Facebook Link copied
Source

Austin Zaback · 5 Side Hustles You Can Start for $500 (Without Quitting Your Job) ↗

What It Is

You buy recovered (used) golf balls in bulk from an authorized source, clean them, sort them by brand and grade, and sell them by the dozen online. The example business sells a dozen mint-condition Titleist Pro V1s for $27.95. The creator suggests bundles of two or three dozen aimed at golfers who keep losing one favourite ball, and spending a couple of evenings a week on cleaning, grading and packing plus weekend time for sourcing and shipping. At $27.95 a dozen, 50 dozen a month is about $1,400 in sales.

The Pitch

The creator says supply is huge, citing a diver who pulls about 70,000 balls a year from a single famous water hole, and that golfers reorder the same ball repeatedly. He points to a family business in this space that appeared on Shark Tank in 2026 and says you can start for about $500 using space you already have.

Best For

Golfers or detail-oriented resellers with a little storage space who are happy to handle physical inventory.

The Truth

The creator stresses that all his figures are sales, not profit; buying cost, packaging and shipping come out of that $27.95. Established sellers already compete in this market, so margins and differentiation (accurate grading, fast shipping) matter. Retrieving balls yourself from course ponds needs the course's permission and can be dangerous, which is why he suggests buying from an authorized source. Selling balls graded higher than their real condition is a quick way to lose customers.
