What It Is

You pick a niche advertisers pay well for (finance, tech or education) and use Higgsfield's Supercomputer with a prompt to find the niche angle, research the topic, write the script, generate the video and make a high click-through thumbnail. You post consistently and make longer videos so you reach YouTube's monetization bar (1,000 subscribers and 4,000 watch hours) faster. He compares two similar-size faceless channels: Casual Finance (about 400,000 subscribers, about $24,000 a month per VidIQ) and Monkey Mind 101 (about 300,000 subscribers, under $1,000 a month). Income comes from three places: other creators who hire you for AI visuals (put a business email in your channel description), sponsorships from about 10,000 subscribers, and AdSense.

The Pitch

The creator says there are no clients to find or manage: "As long as people keep watching your videos, you keep cashing those checks." He says YouTube doesn't demonetize content for being AI-made, only for being boring and unoriginal.

Who It’s Best For

Patient people who can wait months for income and want to stay off camera and away from sales.

The Truth (as we see it)

Gadzhi himself calls this "the slowest burner" of his three methods. The channel earnings he cites are third-party VidIQ estimates for other people's channels, not his results. YouTube doesn't pay for mass-produced or repetitive content, so template-style AI channels often fail to get monetized, which makes his warning about originality important. Getting paid by other creators for AI visuals from a zero-subscriber channel is an idea, not a shown result. Gadzhi says he made a "sizable investment" in Higgsfield, the tool the video is built around, and he offers a free prompt pack and a MacBook giveaway for commenters.

Our assessment, based on the video and what’s typical across similar videos. We don’t independently verify income claims or prices.

Most comments are viewers answering the video's prompt to pick a method (the faceless YouTube option is the clear favorite), along with laptop-giveaway entries, sign-ups for the creator's AI challenge and some book spam. There's no feedback yet from anyone who has made money with these.
