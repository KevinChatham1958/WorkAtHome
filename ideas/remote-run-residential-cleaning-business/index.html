Remote-run Residential Cleaning Business

What It Is

Run a residential house-cleaning business without doing any of the cleaning yourself. Hire independent cleaners who already own their own supplies and vehicles, handle the marketing, scheduling, and customer relationships, and split revenue roughly 50/50 with the cleaner on every job.

The Pitch

The presenters rank this their overall number-one pick because it's a large, fragmented home-services market with no dominant national brand, and roughly one in three one-time cleanings converts into a recurring client, building compounding revenue instead of starting from zero every month. Using a rough example, a $350 first-time deep clean split 50/50 with the cleaner, scaled to around 25 to 30 recurring clients, gets to roughly $10,000 a month in revenue with no employees on payroll and minimal ongoing labor from the owner.

Best For

Someone willing to build the business around recruiting and managing independent cleaners from day one, rather than doing cleanings themselves to save money early on, which the presenters call the single most common way people sabotage this model.

The Truth

The presenters are explicit that this is the business they'd start themselves from zero, and flag a real failure pattern: people who fail at this almost always fail the same way, by getting personally attached to doing the cleaning and building limiting beliefs around recruiting cleaners instead of stepping into an owner role early. They also promote a paid mentorship program at the end with a stated revenue and client guarantee, worth knowing given the number of specific client success figures cited throughout the video.
