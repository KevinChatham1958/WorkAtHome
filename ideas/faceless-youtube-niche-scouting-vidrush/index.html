Spotting Rising YouTube Niches

Faceless YouTube niche-scouting method using recently-pivoted and spiking channels as demand signals

What It Is

Find currently-trending, lower-competition YouTube niches by watching for channels that have recently pivoted their content entirely (a strong signal the old content wasn't working and the new topic is) or channels showing a sudden spike in daily views on simple, low-production videos, then replicate the same proven format (AI voiceover, stock/clip footage, a consistent thumbnail style, built with an AI video tool like VidRush) in a similar or adjacent niche to avoid direct competition.

The Pitch

The presenter walks through roughly 10 real example channels across niches like fallen restaurant/retail chain histories, "we tested X from Walmart" product roundups, royal family content, sports history, AI-avatar-narrated beauty tips for older audiences, weather disaster content, Hollywood then-vs-now, political news, geography facts, and wildlife facts, pointing out which are brand new, which recently pivoted, and which show unusual daily view spikes as evidence the format still works. He notes some of these channels also monetize through digital products linked in the description, not just AdSense.

Who It’s Best For

Someone comfortable with video editing tools (or an AI tool that automates it) and consistent posting, who's willing to research and copy a proven content format into a less crowded niche rather than trying to be original.

The Truth (as we see it)

The core research method — watch for pivots and view spikes as demand signals, then replicate the proven format in an adjacent niche — is a genuinely useful, lower-guesswork approach to picking a content niche, and it echoes similar competitor-modeling advice given by other faceless-YouTube creators. That said, several examples shown are politically or trend-sensitive: a channel about political news the presenter flatly predicts "is 1 million percent going to get banned," and a disaster/weather channel riding a specific real tragedy for views. That's a caution worth taking seriously before copying those specific niches, regardless of current performance, since ad revenue and channel bans in these categories can change quickly and unpredictably.
