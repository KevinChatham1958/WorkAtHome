Spam-update-resistant Rank-and-rent Microsites (unique Pages, Staged Launches)

Marketing & content · Very low startup cost (domain, cheap hosting and an AI subscription)

Jesse Cunningham · AI Websites and Google's Scaled Content Abuse Rules ↗

What It Is

You build and rent out local lead-generation sites, but deliberately avoid the patterns Google's 2026 spam updates target. That means semantically unique service and location pages instead of the same page with the city swapped, staging a site fully before launch rather than dumping pages, publishing at a human pace, real about and contact pages, and original detail such as real price ranges and local references. Each site stands alone (no linked network), and calls are answered 24/7 by a real contractor or an AI agent so visitors engage. Income comes from renting the leads or calls to local businesses.

The Pitch

The creator says Google is "banning slop at scale," not AI, and that sites hit lose 50-80% of traffic or are de-indexed. He sees the crackdown as "a moat, not a wall": lazy templated rank-and-rent sites get wiped out, opening page-one spots for careful operators.

Best For

Suits people already doing or planning AI-built local sites who want to reduce the risk of losing them to search penalties.

The Truth

Much of this video is commentary on Google's spam policies rather than a business walkthrough; the gig is rank-and-rent, and the advice is about protecting it. The guidance on unique content and avoiding page farms matches Google's stated policies, but his traffic-loss percentages are his own estimates. He mentions knowing how to get around third-party AI-content detection but says he only discusses it in his community, which is a red flag worth weighing. He promotes his own paid Skool community (the Rank Expander Academy, 7-day free trial, then paid), and the build blueprint zip file he relies on is only available inside it.
