Sales-Focused AI Agency

Running a $50K/month AI agency for local businesses by focusing entirely on sales and quantified problem-diagnosis, delegating all technical building to developers

What It Is

Build an AI agency selling automation systems (AI receptionist/voice agents, speed-to-lead systems, workflow follow-up automations, database reactivation of dead leads) to unglamorous, high-volume local businesses (roofers, dentists, plumbers, contractors), but personally focus only on sales, delegating 100% of the technical build to hired developers. Filter which problems are worth building for using three questions: does this cost the business money every day it goes unsolved, is it directly tied to revenue, would the result show up on their bottom line within 30 days — if a problem fails any of these, walk away regardless of how technically impressive a solution would be. On sales calls, quantify the specific dollar cost of the business owner's own stated problem in real time (e.g. 15 missed calls a week at an $8,000 average job value, meaning roughly $100,000/month walking out the door), then compare that number directly against your system's price. Be unusually generous with value and specifics on the first call itself rather than gatekeeping details until after signing.

The Pitch

The presenter says he personally cannot code and never touches the backend of any system he sells, scaling to $40,000-50,000 months by spending 100% of his time on sales; he cites a real example client, a roofing company doing $125,000/month with no CRM or follow-up system, who hit $217,000 in their very next month after implementing an AI receptionist and speed-to-lead system, paying $14,000 upfront and later referring two additional clients.

Who It’s Best For

Someone stronger at sales and client-facing diagnosis than at technical building, comfortable hiring and managing developers for fulfillment.

The Truth (as we see it)

The three-question problem filter and the quantified-loss selling technique are sound, well-reasoned B2B sales principles, and the cited Harvard statistic on lead-response-time affecting close rate is a real, independently referenced study (though not sourced within the video itself). The presenter's own revenue figures and the roofing-company case study are self-reported and not independently audited, though the case study is detailed and specific rather than a vague claim.
