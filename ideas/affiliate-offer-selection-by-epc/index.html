Affiliate Offer Selection by Earnings-per-click and Audience Fit, Not Headline Commission

Marketing & content · Free to apply; a keyword-research tool (free or paid) is useful

What It Is

A vetting framework for choosing which affiliate offer to promote to an existing or growing audience: does it solve a problem the audience already told you they have, does it match your traffic's 'temperature,' can you show it rather than just describe it, does it pay once or on repeat, and would you still recommend it if it paid nothing? Emphasizes tracking earnings-per-click (EPC) over chasing the highest advertised commission.

The Pitch

Most beginners pick the highest-payout offer first and then hunt for traffic, which is backwards and explains dead affiliate links that never convert. The presenter's own biggest claimed payday (over $3M in one niche) came from a low per-conversion offer matched to a large volume of the right traffic, not a high-commission offer with little targeted traffic.

Best For

Anyone already promoting or considering affiliate offers to an existing or growing audience, since this is a decision framework to apply rather than a standalone business to start.

The Truth

This is a narrower, tactical companion to the same creator's audience-first philosophy taught elsewhere — kept as its own entry because the seven-question offer filter and EPC framing are a distinct, directly-applicable decision tool rather than a repeat of the domain-buying mechanic. The specific dollar figures cited are the presenter's own unaudited claims, though the general logic (match offer to audience intent, track EPC not sticker commission) is sound, standard practice.
