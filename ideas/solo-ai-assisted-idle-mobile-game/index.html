Niche Mobile Game

Building a small niche mobile game on your own with AI coding help, monetized through optional in-app purchases

  • $500–$2,000 to start

What It Is

Build a small free-to-play mobile game by yourself for a specific niche of players you already understand, using AI coding tools (Cursor, ChatGPT) to write most of the code. Make money through optional in-app purchases that unlock premium features or speed up progress. The featured developer, a former Meta engineering manager who hadn't coded in four years, built a text-based idle RPG for fans of incremental games like RuneScape. He bought ready-made art from asset stores (Unity Asset Store, GameDev Market), hired freelancers on Fiverr/Upwork for icons, screenshots and videos, and ran the backend on Firebase.

The Pitch

The developer reports about $13,000/month on average (over $150,000 in the past year) from only 3,500-4,000 monthly active players, with roughly 50,000 total downloads, a 6% paying conversion rate and about $3 in revenue per active user. His argument is that you don't need a hit game: a small audience that genuinely loves a niche genre can support a full-time income. He spent nothing on ads. His revenue jumped from about $1,000 to $10,000 a month after a single Reddit post, and he found that posts drawing the most criticism brought in the most new players. He also found in-app prices between $4.99 and $9.99 convert best.

Who It’s Best For

Someone who plays and deeply understands a particular game genre, is comfortable learning to build software with AI help, and can accept a slow, uncertain start. It suits people who can keep a day job while testing, as the developer did before quitting.

The Truth (as we see it)

The figures are self-reported in an interview on a channel that promotes indie-builder success stories, and they weren't shown in a way that can be independently checked, though the game itself is real and publicly available. This is one success case; most indie mobile games earn little or nothing, and his result depended heavily on one Reddit post taking off, which he says wasn't consistent. His 12 years as a software engineer at Amazon and Meta almost certainly made the AI-assisted build far easier than it would be for a true beginner. The strategic advice (build for a niche you know, launch early, focus on keeping players before monetizing) is sound and widely shared among indie developers.

Our assessment, based on the video and what’s typical across similar videos. We don’t independently verify income claims or prices.
