Faceless Facts YouTube Shorts

Faceless 'interesting facts' YouTube Shorts channel (Claude + vidIQ research, Viblo editing)

Content & personal brand · Low startup cost (AI and editing subscriptions after free trials)

Originally published Aug 2, 2026 — Kellan Henneberry

Share on Facebook Link copied
Source

Kellan Henneberry · Claude + ChatGPT = $57,152 In 90 Days ↗

What It Is

You run a faceless YouTube Shorts channel of 20-30 second 'interesting facts' videos: an AI voiceover explains a topic over a mix of real clips and AI-generated clips, with bold captions. The creator uses Claude with the vidIQ connector to analyze a successful competitor channel and pull its best-performing topics, then has Claude write a 30-second script and break it into clip-by-clip visual cues. The video is assembled in Viblo (AI voice, AI video generation, importing clips found on TikTok, auto-captions), and ChatGPT creates the channel name, logo, banner and description. The plan is to post one to two Shorts a day, earning from YouTube Shorts ad revenue.

The Pitch

The creator shows a dashboard with about $60,000 profit in 90 days and $236,000 in a year from his own Shorts channel. He argues interest in starting faceless channels is at a low point, so competition is lighter, while Shorts pay per view has roughly quadrupled for him, from about 9 to 38 cents per thousand views. He says AI now handles about 80% of the hard work: research, ideas and scripts.

Who It's Best For

People who can produce short videos daily, are comfortable with AI tools, and are willing to wait for a channel to take off without showing their face.

The Truth (as we see it)

The dashboard numbers are the creator's own and could not be independently verified; results this large come from a small share of channels, and Shorts still pay little per view, so income depends on very high view counts. The method relies on recreating topics from competitors' viral videos and reusing clips from other people's TikToks, which carries copyright and YouTube reused-content risk; the creator's advice to mix in AI clips so viewers don't notice is a reason to read YouTube's disclosure rules carefully. Viblo is promoted repeatedly with a link in the description; the video does not say whether it is sponsored or an affiliate link, so treat that recommendation with some caution.
