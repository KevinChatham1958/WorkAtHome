Freelance Data Entry, Repositioned as a Virtual Assistant Business

Local & professional services · Very low startup cost

Originally published Jul 9, 2026 — Abby Ashley

Share on Facebook Link copied
Source

Abby Ashley · Freelance Data Entry Jobs for Beginners ↗

What It Is

Start with a simple, accessible skill (data entry) but position yourself to clients as a virtual assistant offering a broader menu of "data entry adjacent" tasks (email and calendar management, customer support, invoicing, Canva design, social media scheduling, creating SOPs), sold as recurring monthly hour packages rather than one-off gigs, so a client is paying for ongoing help rather than a single project.

The Pitch

The presenter argues that positioning as a data entry specialist specifically limits your client pool, while positioning as a virtual assistant, even with the same underlying skill set, opens up far more small business owners who need broader ongoing help. A concrete pricing example is given: a student named Jessica built three tiered monthly packages (10, 15, and 20 hours a month) starting at $40/hour, so her lowest tier alone was $400 a month recurring for one client. The suggested growth path is to eventually raise your own rate, then hire team members at a lower rate to actually deliver the work, turning the business into a small agency with a much higher ceiling than trading your own hours.

Best For

Someone comfortable with basic administrative tasks and organized enough to juggle several small recurring clients, who's willing to actively pitch personal networks and small business owners rather than waiting for job board postings.

The Truth

The tiered recurring-package structure and the specific example given (a real dollar figure tied to a real number of hours) is concrete and checkable math, a good sign, though it's one student's account rather than an audited result. The presenter is upfront that generic entry-level data entry postings on marketplaces like Upwork tend to pay poorly, and recommends the VA positioning specifically as the way around that, a reasonably candid acknowledgment of the entry-level market's real limits. The video promotes the presenter's own paid 90-day accelerator program, a financial incentive worth knowing, though the core positioning and pricing strategy doesn't require it to apply.
