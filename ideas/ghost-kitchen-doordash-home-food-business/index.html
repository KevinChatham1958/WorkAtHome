Home Ghost Kitchen on DoorDash

Running a home-based ghost kitchen food business listed on DoorDash

Local & professional services · Low to moderate (ingredients, packaging, and potentially a food handler's permit depending on state)

Originally published Jul 18, 2026 — Diamond Chanel

Share on Facebook Link copied
Source

Diamond Chanel · I Found 7 Boring Side Hustles You Can Run From Your Phone, 24/7 (NOT DoorDash or Uber) ↗

One of 6 ideas covered in this video

What It Is

Prepare a specific, shareable food product from home (the example given is mini cookie cups with a dipping sauce) and list it on DoorDash so local customers can order it, with a driver picking up from your home and delivering it, meaning you never leave the house; depending on your state, this may require a food handler's permit or operating under your state's cottage food law.

The Pitch

The presenter shows a real example of an entrepreneur who went viral selling cookie cups, turning video views into steady weekly orders, and frames a visually distinctive, shareable food product as the key to organic promotion, rating this 3/5 for ease (varies by state regulation) and 2/5 for speed to first dollar since additional promotion beyond DoorDash is usually needed.

Best For

Someone who enjoys cooking/baking and is willing to research and comply with their specific state's home food business regulations.

The Truth

Home-based ghost kitchens listed on delivery apps are a real, growing category, and the video is upfront about the real regulatory requirement (food handler's permit or cottage food law compliance) that varies significantly by state rather than glossing over it. The cited example's viral success is one specific case, not a typical outcome, and building enough visibility for consistent orders takes real marketing effort beyond just listing on DoorDash.
