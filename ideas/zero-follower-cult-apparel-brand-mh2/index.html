Zero-Follower Cult Apparel Brand

Niche apparel brand built from zero followers through seven small trust-building habits in ads, email and weekly drops

  • Online stores
  • $500–$2,000 to start
  • Jump to The Truth ↓

Video published Jul 18, 2026 by Meg Heckman

Meg Heckman · How to Build a Cult like E-commerce Brand with Zero Followers ↗

What It Is

You run a niche t-shirt brand, using Meg's Sloth Hiking Club (shirts for slow, casual hikers) as the model, and earn trust through seven habits. Only release designs a customer might tattoo, meaning ones that finish the sentence 'my customer is the person who...'. Reply to every comment on your ads in the brand's voice (AI can help draft replies). Make the welcome email a short, friendly note that delivers the promised discount and asks one question. Write emails about the customer's life (like the drive home after a hike) with little or no product. Ask subscribers for shirt ideas and send a free shirt when one gets made. Feature customer photos. Release new designs on the same day every week without fail.

The Pitch

She says Sloth Hiking Club launched in January 2025 with no audience and has done over $1.7 million in sales in 18 months, with 84,000 customers and 250-plus designs. Its number-three bestseller came from a subscriber's emailed joke. She cites studies on trust and welcome-email open rates (84%) to argue none of these habits need a following, and most cost nothing.

Who It’s Best For

Writers and people with a sense of humor who know a hobby community well and enjoy talking with customers.

The Truth (as we see it)

The habits are sensible, low-cost customer-relationship practices that any small brand can adopt, and she admits the brand made no profit for its first two months. The sales and customer figures are her own and unverified, and the brand was built by partners who had already run large stores and paid for ads, so 'zero followers' does not mean zero ad budget. The statistics she quotes come from outside studies she names but doesn't link. She promotes her free 12-hour YouTube course and mentions her Skool community.

Our assessment, based on the video and what’s typical across similar videos. We don’t independently verify income claims or prices.
