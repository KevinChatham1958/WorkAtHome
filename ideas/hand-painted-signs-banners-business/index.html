Hand-Painted Signs and Banners

Hand-painting custom signs and party banners as an alternative to vinyl printing

  • Things you make
  • $100–$500 to start
  • Jump to The Truth ↓

Video published Jul 9, 2026 by Austin Zaback

Startup cost: Very low for the banner version (under $200 for paint, brushes, a projector for tracing designs, and Canva for layout); storefront sign painting requires more skill investment but similar low material cost

Share on Facebook Link copied

Source video

Austin Zaback · Side Hustles That Are Making People $100K+/Year (Low Competition) ↗

What It Is

Hand-paint lettering, logos, or artwork instead of using vinyl printing, either as birthday/bachelorette/sweet-16 paper banners (traced with a projector from a Canva design, sold for $60-$120 each, taking under an hour per banner) or, at a more skilled level, as storefront and mural signage sold directly to businesses for hundreds to tens of thousands of dollars.

The Pitch

The presenter cites two examples: Alex Slabik, who built a following of nearly 600,000 selling hand-painted party banners via Instagram/TikTok DMs with no website or ad spend, and Will Van Z, who hand-paints and gold-leaf-gilds storefront signage for restaurants and shops in New York. He frames vinyl printing's dominance as having created an opening for the hand-crafted alternative.

Who It’s Best For

Someone with (or willing to develop) hand-lettering/painting skill, comfortable building an audience by documenting the painting process on social media to drive DM-based orders.

The Truth (as we see it)

The core observation, that hand-craftsmanship stands out against mass-produced vinyl printing and can support a real following-driven sales channel, is sound, and the banner version genuinely has a very low cost of entry. The follower-count and pricing figures come from two specific individual examples rather than typical outcomes, and building an audience large enough to sustain consistent DM orders took real, sustained content effort, not just posting a few paintings.

Our assessment, based on the video and what’s typical across similar videos. We don’t independently verify income claims or prices.
