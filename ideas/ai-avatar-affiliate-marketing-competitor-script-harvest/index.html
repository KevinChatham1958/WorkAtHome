AI Avatar Affiliate Videos

Faceless AI avatar affiliate marketing for Amazon-listed brands (competitor-script research method)

Eric Swanson · I Work 2 Hours a Day But This AI Business Makes Me $10K/mo ↗

What It Is

Create a synthetic AI "spokesperson" character and post short-form videos as that character on Facebook and Instagram (not TikTok, which the presenter argues penalizes AI content and pays far less), promoting existing physical products via an Amazon Associates affiliate link, with income tracked and paid out through the brand's own affiliate program rather than through the social platform itself. The specific research method described: follow a niche's existing AI-avatar accounts from a secondary account, identify videos with unusually high view counts relative to how recently they were posted, extract the transcript, and feed several of these into Claude to identify a common winning script structure and character archetype to replicate.

The Pitch

The core argument for this channel mix over TikTok Shop is stability and payout structure: since Instagram and Facebook reward organic content rather than requiring paid promotion, a well-performing video keeps earning views for weeks without ad spend, and because the brand pays the affiliate directly rather than through the platform, a account ban doesn't freeze already-earned commissions the way a TikTok Shop suspension can. The presenter reports scaling from two total sales in his first two months to consistent five-figure months by the following February, largely by outsourcing video production to a single hired assistant for $400 a month.

Who It’s Best For

Someone comfortable directing (rather than personally performing) content production and willing to hire and manage outsourced help early on, since the presenter's own stated workflow no longer includes making the videos himself.

The Truth (as we see it)

This needs a direct, upfront caution: this technique is demonstrated using health and wellness supplement products, and the video explicitly warns viewers not to build a character claiming to be a doctor, an implicit acknowledgment that some in this space do exactly that. Building any fabricated persona to sell a health-related product without disclosing it's synthetic, especially one implying medical or specialized health authority, raises real deceptive-advertising concerns that go well beyond a normal AI-content disclaimer, and is worth thinking through carefully regardless of the underlying affiliate-marketing mechanic's legitimacy. This entry shares its core mechanic (AI avatar, Amazon Associates, brand-direct commissions via a paid creator community) with another entry in this database that emphasizes a different specific research technique (mining Amazon product reviews rather than competitor scripts); both carry the same health-marketing caution. The video promotes the presenter's own paid creator community as the path to brand-deal access.
