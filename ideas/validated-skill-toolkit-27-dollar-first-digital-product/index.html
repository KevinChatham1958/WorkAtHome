What It Is

List what people already ask you about, what you have been paid or praised for, and what you learned the hard way, then test each idea with three checks: someone already sells it with reviews, people complain about the problem on Reddit or in comments, and companies pay for the skill in job postings. Pick one narrow buyer (his example is a spreadsheet modeling toolkit for people starting their first finance job), use an AI tool to outline lessons by outcome, and fill them with your own experience and examples. Lay it out simply in Canva or Word with a 'start here' page that gives a win in 10 minutes, sell it through a checkout that emails the file automatically (he uses Wix with Stripe or PayPal), and test-buy it yourself. Find first buyers by posting short tips, offering a free checklist to anyone who comments a keyword, and having real conversations in messages before sharing the link.

The Pitch

The creator says you do the work once and every later sale costs nothing, and that you only need to be ahead of the person you are helping, not an expert. He says his first product, a $500 productivity course for college students, made over $500,000 in eight months, and recommends starting under $50 to collect buyers and testimonials before building pricier offers.

Who It’s Best For

People with a practical skill from work or life who are willing to write it up and talk one-on-one with potential buyers.

The Truth (as we see it)

This is a clear, grounded walk-through, and the emphasis on validating demand before building and on making honest promises is good advice. His $500,000 figure is his own and unverified, and it came with a YouTube channel that grew past 100,000 subscribers, which most beginners won't have. A $27 product needs many sales to add up, and the first buyers usually come from the time-consuming one-on-one conversations he describes. No paid product is pitched here; he points viewers to a free masterclass on his channel.
