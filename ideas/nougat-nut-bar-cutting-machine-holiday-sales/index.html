Artisanal Nougat and Bars

Artisanal nougat and nut bars made with a forming-and-cutting machine

What It Is

A stainless steel machine spreads a mix of sugar, honey, nuts and seeds evenly across a sheet and slices it into uniform bars, lengthwise and across. The same setup makes cereal bars, peanut bars, sesame bars, energy bars and traditional grain candies. The creator says a nougat bar costs around 50 cents to make and sells for $4 to $12 with decent packaging.

The Pitch

The creator says nougat demand triples or quadruples during the holidays while still selling the rest of the year, which makes it a strong seasonal business. Uniform bars give handmade treats a professional shelf look.

Who It’s Best For

Home bakers or candy makers who already have a product people like and want consistent, giftable packaging.

The Truth (as we see it)

The cost-to-price gap is plausible for premium confectionery, but those prices depend on good packaging and a brand people trust. Home-made candy falls under cottage food rules, which in many places limit where you can sell (often direct to consumers only) and may cap revenue; shipping across state lines or selling to stores can require a licensed kitchen. Spending $5,650 before testing demand is a big step, so many makers would test small batches by hand first. Sales are seasonal.
