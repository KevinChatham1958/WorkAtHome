What It Is

Rather than quoting each project or billing by the hour, sell a fixed-scope marketing service with set deliverables and posted prices, often on a three-tier pricing page. Examples include a 90-day LinkedIn content calendar with 60 posts written and scheduled for about $1,000, a complete email funnel for a course creator (welcome, abandoned-cart and post-purchase emails, set up and tested) for $2,000 to $3,000, or a monthly short-form video editing package.

The Pitch

Enfroy says fixed packages remove decision fatigue for buyers, who can pick a tier without explaining their whole situation to get custom quotes. He positions it as sitting between software and an agency: buyers comparing you to agencies charging $5,000 to $10,000 see a clearer, cheaper $2,000 to $3,000 package. He says he himself paid $6,000 a month for a package that turned his raw videos into 30 edited, captioned and uploaded shorts. His math: five clients at $3,000 a month is $15,000 a month. He says the first client might take 10 hours to work out and the second about two, because you document the process as you go.

Who It’s Best For

Someone who is already good at one specific marketing task (copywriting, email setup, video editing, LinkedIn content) and wants steadier, easier-to-sell work than custom freelance quotes.

The Truth (as we see it)

This is a well-proven way to run a service business, and Enfroy's $6,000-a-month purchase is a concrete sign that buyers exist. It is still client work, though: you have to find buyers, deliver every month, and keep the scope from creeping. The revenue figures are illustrations, and he gives no results of his own from selling packages like this. The video promotes Enfroy's free "AI launch kit" lead magnet, which feeds his own paid programs.
