Bulk Clothing Reselling

Bulk clothing reselling sourced from raw-credential donation loads

David Pelayo · Exactly How To Find The Best Clothing Wholesale Supplier (No Gatekeeping) ↗

What It Is

Instead of hunting thrift stores and Goodwill bins piece by piece, you buy used clothing in bulk as 'raw credential' (also called raw donation) - unsorted loads sold by the pound or by the truckload from the companies that run clothing donation bins and from rag houses at the top of the resale supply chain. You sort the load yourself: the best pieces (roughly 20-30%) get listed on eBay at around a $30 average, and the rest is resold in bulk on Facebook Marketplace or to local shops and exporters so little is wasted. Pelayo says the key is finding a supplier who will sell you raw loads and building a repeatable process to sort, grade, photograph and list quickly.

The Pitch

Pelayo says sourcing one item at a time caps you at a few thousand dollars a month and burns days of your week, while buying raw puts you 'at the top of the market' at a far lower cost per item and lets the business scale; he claims students have gone from about $1,200 to $16,000 a month in six months and that he has processed over a million pounds of clothing in a year. He argues you're really competing with Goodwill's billion-dollar sorting operation, and buying raw is how a small seller gets similar leverage.

Who It’s Best For

Resellers who already understand online selling, can commit space (a garage or storage unit at minimum), can handle sorting large volumes of mixed goods, and want to scale past piece-by-piece thrifting.

The Truth (as we see it)

The supply-chain explanation is detailed and credible, and bulk sourcing is a real way experienced resellers scale. But the barriers are higher than the video lets on: finding a legitimate raw-credential supplier is genuinely hard (he admits some people never do), loads require storage, labor and up-front cash, and only a fraction of each load is prime eBay stock, so profit depends on also monetizing the lower-tier excess at low margins. Pelayo sells wholesale loads himself and promotes his paid 'Inventory Operating System' program, and the student income figures are his own and unaudited. This suits someone ready to run a real inventory operation, not a casual side hustle.

Our assessment, based on the video and what’s typical across similar videos. We don’t independently verify income claims or prices.
