AI Workshop From Your Expertise

Leveraging existing niche industry expertise into a cheap AI workshop and paid assessment funnel

AI & automation · Very low startup cost (an existing niche network/email list; AI tools for the deliverable)

Originally published Aug 26, 2026 — Corey Ganim

Corey Ganim · Landing a $30K Retainer BRAND NEW to AI (Case Study) ↗

What It Is

Use existing deep knowledge of a specific industry (not AI or technical skill) to run a cheap, niche-specific AI workshop for that industry's professionals, then upsell attendees into a paid, personalized AI assessment (a written report plus a short AI-generated explainer video walking through their specific numbers and pain points), positioning the assessment as a self-serve blueprint they could implement themselves or hire the presenter's company to execute for a much larger retainer.

The Pitch

The guest, an orthodontic-practice consultant with no coding or technical background, ran a $97 workshop (21 people, some at an early-bird $49 rate) teaching a simple 3-part filter for evaluating AI opportunities, then sold a $497-997 personalized assessment where he used Claude to generate a report and a short Notebook LM/Gemini explainer video referencing the client's specific numbers (e.g., an EBITDA target for a practice sale). One assessment client signed a $30,000, 6-month retainer after seeing the report, reasoning it was easier to pay him than spend 200 hours implementing it herself.

Best For

Someone with genuine, established expertise and trust within a specific niche or industry (even without any AI or technical background), willing to package that trust into a structured workshop-then-assessment funnel.

The Truth

The mechanic is a compelling example of how existing industry credibility, not technical skill, can be the actual unlock for AI consulting — the presenter is explicit he can't code and isn't a developer. The specific numbers ($97 workshop, $497 assessment, $30k retainer) reflect one strong case study from a coach's own student rather than a typical or guaranteed outcome, and success still depends on already having (or being willing to build) real credibility within a specific niche.
