Photo and VHS Digitizing

Photo scanning and VHS-to-digital conversion done at home

Local & professional services · Moderate startup cost (about $150 for a photo scanner and VHS converter, plus a used VCR)

What It Is

Customers bring you boxes of old photos and VHS tapes, and you digitize them at home with a photo scanner, a VHS-to-digital converter and a used VCR (about $20 at thrift stores). The creator cites common rates of $1-2 per photo (a 200-photo shoebox brings $200-400) and $25-40 per tape. You return files on a USB drive (about $5 each) or an online link for an extra $10.

The Pitch

The creator says the equipment pays for itself after one or two orders, that tapes record while you do other things, and that people do not haggle much over saving family memories. He suggests flyers at churches and senior centers and posts in local groups, since older people often have the most tapes.

Who It's Best For

Patient, careful people who want a quiet home service with local customers, including retirees.

The Truth (as we see it)

Digitizing is a genuine service with steady demand, and the price ranges are in line with what local services commonly charge. Large national mail-in services compete on price, so local trust and convenience are your edge. Tapes take real time (an hour of tape is an hour of recording) and old tapes can be fragile, so set expectations. The creator sells a paid 'roadmap' guide for starting a home-based machine business (supplier info, budgets, calculators, outreach templates), linked in the description.
