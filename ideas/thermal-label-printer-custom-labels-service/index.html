Thermal Label Printing

Custom labels and stickers made with a thermal label printer for local shops and small sellers

What It Is

A thermal label printer uses heat instead of ink, so each label costs a few cents. The creator suggests using it to brand your own products, printing barcodes, jar labels or name tags for local shops at $20-40 per batch, or offering to print and prepare shipping for small Etsy and eBay sellers at 50 cents to $1 per package.

The Pitch

The creator says it is one of the easiest machines to start with, sets up in about 10 minutes, and makes any product look more professional, which can justify charging $1-3 more per item.

Who It's Best For

People who want a cheap add-on service or already sell their own products and want professional labeling.

The Truth (as we see it)

Thermal printers are cheap and useful, but standard thermal labels are black-only and can fade, so they suit shipping and simple labels more than premium product branding. Labeling as a stand-alone service is a thin business; most small sellers can buy the same $80 printer. The package-prep service would also require sellers to hand you their orders. Figures are estimates. The creator sells a paid 'roadmap' guide for starting a home-based machine business (supplier info, budgets, calculators, outreach templates), linked in the description.
