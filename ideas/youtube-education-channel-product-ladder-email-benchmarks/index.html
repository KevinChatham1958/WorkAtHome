Educational YouTube Channel Business

Problem-solving YouTube channel that grows an email list, then sells a ladder of own offers (calls, low-ticket product, workshop, cohort, high-ticket)

What It Is

You pick one specific problem in your niche to "own", study what gets clicks by scrolling the YouTube home page daily, and make educational videos built on proven topics with your own twist, aiming for the home page rather than search. Once videos reliably get about 1,000 views in the first week, you add a simple email signup page and start emailing monthly at 300 subscribers, weekly at 1,000. When you reach roughly 3,000 views per video, 1,000 subscribers and about 50% open rates, you start selling: paid one-hour calls (he went from $195 to $3,000 an hour), a low-priced product, a 90-minute workshop (his sold for $149), then a live 10-week cohort (around $1,900) and possibly a high-ticket program. Income comes from your own offers, not ad revenue, sponsors or affiliate links.

The Pitch

The creator says he made over $4 million from YouTube in three years with this system and that 40 creators he tested it on averaged $10,000 in their first week of launching. He argues owning a narrow problem makes you the first name people think of, and that email list sales are where the money is, citing a $300,000 week from four emails.

Who It’s Best For

People with real expertise who enjoy teaching on camera and can commit to about three years of steady content before expecting meaningful income.

The Truth (as we see it)

The advice to build an audience and email list before launching products, with clear milestones, is sound, and he is frank that the first year may earn nothing. His income and student results are his own, unaudited and likely the best cases. Being on camera is central, and the higher-priced offers involve live calls and cohort sessions. He sells a high-ticket program for YouTubers, and the video ends with a sponsor read for a sales-tracking tool.

Our assessment, based on the video and what’s typical across similar videos. We don’t independently verify income claims or prices.
