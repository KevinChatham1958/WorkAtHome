Webinar Script Writing Service

What It Is

You write the full sales presentation, usually a webinar, that a client reads to win customers - bookkeepers, lawyers, doctors, insurance agents and similar professionals rather than course sellers. The guest, copywriter Dan Henry, builds each script around one core belief: a 'big idea' that reframes the buyer's problem, a 'unique mechanism' for solving it, and the client's service as the easiest way to use that mechanism, ending with an offer to simply do it for the viewer. Research is the heavy lifting: he asks clients for sales-call transcripts, CRM data and pre-webinar survey answers, and uses AI to pull out pain points and objections, but writes the script himself. Scripts are revised in versions as new call data comes in, and he favors paid webinars over free ones because people who pay stay to the pitch.

The Pitch

Henry argues that many professionals are excellent at their work but poor at explaining it, so a well-written presentation can multiply their new business. He cites a Canadian bookkeeper who went from about $3,000-4,000 a month in new clients to a $47,000 month after he wrote her a webinar, and says his own rewrites of his presentation took his company from about $200,000 to $950,000 cash collected in a month. Host Ryan Pineda adds that he uses webinars in his own tax firm and golf club to win back prospects who didn't buy on a sales call.

Who It’s Best For

Strong persuasive writers who enjoy studying direct-response copy and digging through customer data, and who are comfortable selling a high-ticket service to business owners.

The Truth (as we see it)

Writing sales presentations for other businesses is a real, established copywriting niche, and the framework here (one core belief, objection research from sales calls, testing versions) is more concrete than most interviews offer. But the income figures - $950,000 in a month, $45 million over 13 years, the bookkeeper's $47,000 month - are Henry's own unaudited claims, and his results came with an ad budget, a sales team and 13 years of experience, so a newcomer should expect to start with small clients at modest fees. Much of this two-hour episode is a debate about Dubai and US taxes rather than how-to. Henry sells his own done-for-you webinar service, a paid workshop, a mastermind and private consulting days, and Pineda plugs his own agency, tax firm and golf mastermind during the show.
