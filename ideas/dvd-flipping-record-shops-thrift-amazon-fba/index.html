DVD Reselling on Amazon

Reselling used and sealed DVDs bought at record shops and thrift stores, sent to Amazon FBA

  • Online stores
  • Under $100 to start
  • Jump to The Truth ↓

Video published Nov 24, 2026 by The Used Book Guy

Startup cost: Low startup cost (Amazon seller account, scanning app, first DVDs at about $1-5 each)

Share on Facebook Link copied

Source video

The Used Book Guy · How To Make Thousands Selling DVDs Online! ↗

What It Is

You go to record shops and thrift stores, scan DVD and Blu-ray barcodes with a sourcing app (he uses Ranger, plus a Bluetooth ring scanner), and buy only the discs the app shows will profit after Amazon's fees. Back home you list them, mostly as used, and ship them in boxes to Amazon's warehouses, where Amazon stores, sells and delivers them; he ships three to seven boxes a week. Typical finds cost $1-5 and sell for $10-30, with sealed copies, TV box sets and seasonal titles doing best. You can also sell on eBay, consign to another seller if you have no Amazon account, or rebuy past best-sellers online to resell.

The Pitch

The creator says the barcode data removes the guesswork, and that record shops have cleaner, more niche stock than thrift stores. He shows his own figures: 954 DVDs sold in the past 12 months (about $6,500 profit by his estimate) and about $17,000 profit over two years, working one sourcing day a week. He argues $500 to several thousand a month is possible if you treat it as a business.

Who It’s Best For

Organized bargain hunters who enjoy store-hopping, don't mind packing boxes weekly and have room to hold a small amount of stock.

The Truth (as we see it)

This is a real, long-running reselling model, and his numbers are shown on screen, though they are his own and unaudited; note that roughly half of each sale goes to Amazon fees, and profit per disc is small, so volume matters. He admits it is harder in 2026 than in 2025. The paid work starts with driving to stores to source, so it is not fully at home. He promotes his $2.99-a-month YouTube membership and Discord, plus discount or referral links for the listing and inventory tools he uses. Watch for bootleg copies, which he warns about.

Our assessment, based on the video and what’s typical across similar videos. We don’t independently verify income claims or prices.
