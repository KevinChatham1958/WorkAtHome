Pay-Per-Appointment Contractor Leads

Pay-per-appointment lead generation for home-service contractors, with GoHighLevel and an AI follow-up bot

  • Marketing for businesses
  • $100–$500 to start
  • Jump to The Truth ↓

Video published Nov 3, 2026 by Oliver Rasmussen

Startup cost: Low to moderate startup cost (GoHighLevel subscription and outreach tools; clients cover their own ad spend) (our estimate; the video doesn’t give figures)

Source video

Oliver Rasmussen · How This 20-Year-Old Made $74K With AI ↗

What It Is

You sell leads and booked appointments to local trades such as kitchen and bathroom remodelers, roofers, landscapers and painters. In this interview the 20-year-old founder started with small website jobs on Upwork, then moved to a results-based offer: the contractor pays the ad spend, you run the ads, an AI assistant follows up and books the calls, and you charge about $150-$250 per booked appointment with a $1 starting fee. Clients are found by cold text messages (cold SMS) and sales calls, and you upsell simple GoHighLevel add-ons such as a website, Google review requests, a chatbot or a basic CRM. Once revenue is steady, the plan is to charge about $1,000+ upfront and later reinvest profit into paid ads to find clients nationwide.

The Pitch

The guest says small businesses always want more customers and bigger ones want to cut time and staff costs, so demand will not run out. He argues that a 'no upfront risk' offer gets far more yeses on sales calls, and he shows Stripe screens he says went from a first $90 Upwork job after eight months to about $9,000-$13,000 a month.

Who It’s Best For

Someone comfortable with cold outreach, sales calls and learning paid ads, who can handle client pressure when leads don't turn up.

The Truth (as we see it)

Pay-per-appointment offers are real, but they carry risk: if the ads or the follow-up underperform, you have done the work and earn little, and disputes over what counts as a 'qualified' appointment are common. The income figures and Stripe screens are the guest's own and not independently checked, and his first eight months earned almost nothing, which is the more typical story. Cold texting businesses is regulated in many places (in the US, under telemarketing and texting rules), so check the law before you start. The host promotes his own '7-day challenge' training in the description, which leads into his paid AI-agency program.

Our assessment, based on the video and what’s typical across similar videos. We don’t independently verify income claims or prices.
