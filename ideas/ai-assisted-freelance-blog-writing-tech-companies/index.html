B2B SaaS Blog Writing

Freelance writing AI-assisted, SEO-focused blog articles for B2B SaaS companies at $900-1,200 per piece

  • Under $100 to start

What It Is

Write long-form (2,500-3,000 word), AI-assisted but substantially human-crafted blog articles for B2B SaaS companies (app comparisons, 'alternatives to X' pieces, step-by-step tutorials) at $900-1,200 per article, targeting keywords the client provides so no independent SEO research is required beyond standard best practices. Use different AI models for different jobs within one workflow (one model for processing video/audio notes, another for matching a specific brand voice, a coding-focused AI tool as the connective router between them) rather than relying on a single model for everything, and always keep a substantial personal editing/voice pass rather than publishing AI output directly. Find clients primarily through Upwork (proactively applying to a target number of jobs per day, selecting ones with growth potential), and separately, build a portfolio by writing genuine, unsolicited example pieces for brands you actually like and pitching them directly, which sidesteps the usual chicken-and-egg problem of needing client work to get client work.

The Pitch

The presenter interviews a real, named freelance writer who left her full-time job at Zapier three weeks before this recording to freelance full-time, having built her rates from a standing start on Upwork in 2016 to her current $900-1,200 per blog post; she also describes personally hiring several other real freelance writers on Upwork just to interview them about how newcomers break into the field, relaying their shared advice as a second layer of validation.

Who It’s Best For

Someone with genuine writing/editing skill and some subject-matter interest, comfortable using multiple AI tools as drafting assistants, and willing to proactively apply to jobs on Upwork rather than waiting to be found.

The Truth (as we see it)

This is a real, named interviewee describing her own specific rates, client roster, and workflow in detail, which is more verifiable than an anonymous case study, and the broader claim (companies paying a premium for AI-assisted human writing) lines up with a real, cited industry data point (an internal Zapier report finding AI comprised only about 18% of a typical successful content workflow, always with a human in the loop). No aggregate revenue figure is given for the presenter himself since this is someone else's business being profiled; the video includes a brief paid-community plug near the end, incidental rather than central.

Our assessment, based on the video and what’s typical across similar videos. We don’t independently verify income claims or prices.
