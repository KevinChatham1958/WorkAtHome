Simple Utility Tool Websites Monetized With Display Ads

AI & automation · Low startup cost

AI website-building YouTube creator · This Boring Website Earns $6M/Year. Here's How to Clone it (No Coding!) ↗

What It Is

Build simple, single-purpose utility web tools (a word unscrambler, a calculator, a generator) using a no-code AI website builder, then monetize purely through Google AdSense display ads, which pay based on page views shown, not clicks. The video demonstrates building a working word-unscrambler clone from scratch, in plain English prompts, in about 15 minutes.

The Pitch

The pitch leans on real, checkable examples of this model at scale: a word-unscrambler site pulling an estimated 10-11 million monthly visitors (via the free SimilarWeb Chrome extension) and, at a plausible $9 RPM (earnings per 1,000 page views), roughly $495,000 a month in pure ad revenue. Unlike content businesses that require constant new output, a utility tool solves one evergreen problem and, once built and ranked, keeps running with essentially no maintenance. The suggested research method is specific and checkable: use a keyword tool to find low-competition search terms, then check whether Google is already ranking new, low-trust-score sites for that term, since that's a signal the established competition isn't fully answering the query yet.

Best For

Someone comfortable with basic SEO research and willing to build and rank multiple small tools (the video recommends a portfolio approach, since any single tool's earnings are unpredictable) rather than betting everything on one site.

The Truth

The RPM-based revenue estimate for the example site is a calculation from public traffic data and a typical ad-rate range, not a confirmed number from the site's own analytics, so treat the $495,000-a-month figure as illustrative math rather than a verified result. The video is a direct demonstration of, and funnel toward, a specific paid AI website-building tool the presenter promotes throughout (including a referral-commission program for that same tool), a real financial incentive, though the underlying utility-site-plus-AdSense model doesn't require that specific tool to work. Update: a later, more skeptical source video directly examining this genre made a strong, specific case worth weighing seriously. It pointed out that this exact "boring website earns millions, here's how to clone it" video format has been recycled by many different creators with near-identical titles and thumbnails, and that in every version examined, the tutorial itself doubled as an affiliate funnel for a specific paid hosting/website-building tool, meaning the video's real, reliable income is the presenter's affiliate commission, not the cloned utility site's ad revenue. It also raised a concrete, checkable objection to the core pitch: Google ranks new sites partly on domain trust and authority built over years, so a newly published calculator or generator is competing against established, trusted sites (often themselves attached to a bank, a well-known blog, or another authoritative source) for the same search terms, making it genuinely difficult, not simply a matter of building the tool, to ever rank well enough to get meaningful traffic. Its suggested alternative: build a narrow, specific tool for one real paying business client who already needs it, rather than a generic public tool competing for broad search traffic. Worth reading both perspectives before starting: the underlying model can work, but the ranking difficulty and the affiliate-funnel structure behind many of these tutorials are real, specific caveats, not just generic skepticism.
