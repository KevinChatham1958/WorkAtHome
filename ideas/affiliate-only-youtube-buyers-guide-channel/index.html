Affiliate-only YouTube Buyer's-guide Channel (reviews, Versus Videos, 'best X' Lists)

Marketing & content · Low startup cost

Adam Enfroy · The Complete AI YouTube Blueprint for 2026 (3 Hour Course) ↗

What It Is

Run a simple YouTube channel whose only job is sending buyers to affiliate links, with no course or product of your own. Every video is one of four buyer-intent formats: a single-product review, a head-to-head comparison, a how-to tutorial where one step is 'use this tool', or a 'best X' list of five to seven options. You film yourself talking to a camera or recording your screen, and the video description holds your affiliate links.

The Pitch

Enfroy says YouTube is easier to rank on than Google in competitive niches. Searches like 'best firm mattress' are crowded with big media sites on Google but show low competition in the keyword tool VidIQ, and a new video can get views within days instead of the three to twelve months SEO takes. He says ad revenue has been only about 2% of his own channel's income, and that 50 modest videos at 1,000 monthly views each add up to 50,000 views a month without anything going viral. He points to a 'how to make a website' video with about 500,000 views whose first description link is a Squarespace affiliate link, and to his own Bluehost review, which has earned about 13,000 views over time. His tactics: put the main link on the top line of the description with a discount hook, repeat it in a pinned comment for phone viewers, mention it out loud within the first two minutes with an on-screen caption, and in broad hobby niches paste the same list of about ten common gear links into every description.

Best For

People who know a product category well (software, finance, outdoor gear, cameras) and are comfortable explaining products on camera or over a screen recording, who want affiliate income without ever building a course.

The Truth

The four video formats and the description tactics are standard, believable affiliate-YouTube practice, and the example videos he shows are real and checkable. The view-stacking math is his own illustration, not a typical result, and how much a view earns depends heavily on commission size, which he admits is much higher for software and finance than for cheap Amazon items. You also have to own or get access to whatever you review. You must add an affiliate disclosure to every video. The '2026 blueprint' is stitched together from older course lessons (one part says 'right now, at least late 2023', and there are guest segments with co-host Colin and a team member, Eddie), and it repeatedly promotes Enfroy's paid YouTube Growth Engine course, his AI master class, VidIQ and StreamYard. A lot of it overlaps with his other YouTube advice. What it adds is the affiliate-only, no-course approach and the buyer-intent formats.
