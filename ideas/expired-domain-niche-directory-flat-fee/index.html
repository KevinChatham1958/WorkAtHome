Expired-Domain Niche Directory

Niche directory built on a bought expired domain, charging advertisers a flat yearly listing fee

Digital & subscription products · Moderate startup cost (the guest paid about $18,000 for a premium domain; cheaper domains exist)

What It Is

Watch expiring-domain auctions (GoDaddy, NameJet, SnapNames, DropCatch, or the free search site expireddomains.net) for descriptive names in a niche with real search volume and high-ticket customers, then build a directory on it. The guest bought DudeRanch.com for just under $18,000 because families spend $10,000 to $30,000 on a dude-ranch vacation, then charged ranches a flat $575 a year to be listed, with no cut of bookings. About 60 advertisers bring in a little over $50,000 a year, and he says it now takes about an hour a month, with type-in traffic from people typing the domain directly giving it an early boost.

The Pitch

The guest says a strong descriptive domain brings traffic from day one without relying on paid search, and that directories with real photos and information are still growing in the AI era (his traffic is up about 39% year over year).

Who It's Best For

Patient people who enjoy a niche, can do basic WordPress work, and are willing to sell listings to business owners.

The Truth (as we see it)

Domain-led directories can work well, but this example rests on an expensive domain and many years of building; most expired domains bring little traffic, and auction prices for good ones are high. The hour-a-month figure describes the mature site, not the build-up. Selling listings means reaching out to business owners. The interview is with a guest on the host's highlights channel; figures are the guest's own and unaudited, and the projects took years to build.
