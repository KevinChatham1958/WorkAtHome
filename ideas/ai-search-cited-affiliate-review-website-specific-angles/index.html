AI-Cited Affiliate Review Website

Product review affiliate website built with an AI site builder, targeting narrow 'best X for Y' searches so Google's AI answers cite it

What It Is

You build a product review website in a proven buying niche (his example is home fitness gear) and earn affiliate commissions when readers click through and buy. The creator's five steps: test the niche by checking whether Google's AI answer cites review sites; build the site with an AI website builder; find narrow searches the big sites ignore (like 'best adjustable dumbbells for seniors') using Google autocomplete, the AI answer's follow-up questions and the big sites' topic lists; write 'quote-ready' articles with a labeled pick for each type of buyer, a summary of what real buyer reviews say, a comparison table, a visible updated date and an FAQ; and link the buy buttons through Amazon Associates or brands' own affiliate programs. He drafts the facts himself and has Claude or ChatGPT turn his notes into the article, with an instruction not to add facts.

The Pitch

The creator argues affiliate sites aren't dead because Google's AI answers have to borrow recommendations from websites and show them as sources, which still sends clicks. He says big sites skip narrow searches, so a new site can be the best answer for a specific buyer. His illustration is an article with 2,000 monthly visitors at 3% conversion and $5 per sale making about $300 a month, and he stresses those are example numbers, not a promise.

Who It’s Best For

Patient writers who like researching products and can keep publishing for several months before seeing much traffic.

The Truth (as we see it)

The method itself (narrow buyer-intent articles, honest picks, comparison tables, regular updates) is sound and is how many small review sites have earned money. The creator is upfront that it takes months before Google trusts a new site and that his income example is hypothetical; real results after Google's AI changes vary widely and many small affiliate sites have lost traffic in recent years. Amazon's commission rates are low in many categories, so the brands' own programs matter. Disclosure: the video is sponsored by the website builder he recommends, and he uses an affiliate link and personal coupon code for it.
