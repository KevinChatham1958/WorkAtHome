High-Volume Depop Reselling

High-volume Depop clothing reselling, scaled with daily listing quotas, cross-listing and social media

  • Under $100 to start
  • Jump to The Truth ↓

Startup cost: Very low startup cost (start with your own closet and cheap thrift finds) (our estimate; the video doesn’t give figures)

Evan Zoeteman · How I Scaled My Depop Business From $0 to $10K a Month ↗

What It Is

You source second-hand clothing from thrift stores and the bins, photograph it on a flat-lay setup, list it on Depop and ship each order yourself. The creator started by selling cheap $3 to $10 items from his own closet and from friends, then mixed in higher-priced $20-plus pieces as he improved his photos and sourcing. He sets daily numbers (source, list and ship 20, then 30, then more items a day), lists one item every two to three minutes, and aims for 10,000 active listings, which he says supports about $1,000 a day. He now cross-lists every item to eBay, Poshmark and Mercari with listing software, and promotes his shop through several Instagram, Facebook, Pinterest, Threads, TikTok and YouTube accounts. He says he has 32,000 sales, earns around $10,000 a month, and once hit $24,000 in a December.

The Pitch

The creator says the numbers do not lie: more listings mean more sales, and a big shop gives every visitor more chances to find something. He says selling low-priced items keeps sales steady while higher-priced pieces lift profit, that small time savings in listing and packing add up over thousands of orders, and that free social media posting is the best advertising a reseller has.

Who It’s Best For

Energetic resellers who like thrifting and fashion, can put in full-time hours, and have space to store a large stock of clothing.

The Truth (as we see it)

The method (volume, daily quotas, efficient listing, cross-listing) is standard reselling advice and believable, but the income figures are his own and unaudited, and he does not say what is left after sourcing costs, fees and shipping supplies. He works 10 to 16 hours a day, so $10,000 a month here reflects a full-time-plus job, not a side hustle. He says Depop has become harder because the platform now favors casual sellers over resellers, which pushed him to cut prices and cross-list, and he calls single-platform selling a business without much longevity. No paid product of his own is promoted in this video.

Our assessment, based on the video and what’s typical across similar videos. We don’t independently verify income claims or prices.
