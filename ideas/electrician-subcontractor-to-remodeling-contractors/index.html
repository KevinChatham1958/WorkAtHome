Building an Electrical (or Trade) Business as a Trusted Subcontractor to Remodeling General Contractors, Rather Than Chasing Homeowners Directly

Local & professional services · Low (licensing costs assumed for the trade; primary investment is time spent prospecting and building relationships)

Originally published Sep 8, 2026 — David Heacock

David Heacock · Copy This Strategy, You'll Never Be Broke Again (99% Success Rate) ↗

What It Is

Instead of marketing directly to homeowners, build a trade business around becoming the trusted subcontractor for general contractors doing remodels: research and shortlist reputable local contractors, reach out directly (cold calls preferred over email) to the right decision-maker, prove yourself on a test job, and grow a repeat-client base of contractors who send consistent work; to scale beyond personal capacity, build a systematic list of target contractors (e.g. by researching who's pulling relevant permits in your area, or using B2B contact tools like Apollo or ZoomInfo) rather than randomly prospecting day to day.

The Pitch

The presenter (running a business-coaching show) interviews an electrician who quit a stable $83K-100K/year job to start his own electrical business 18 months prior with no client base, diagnosing his current bottleneck (he personally does all the sales, capping how much work he can take on) and mapping out a systematic prospecting process to scale beyond solo capacity.

Best For

Someone with an existing trade license/skill willing to build relationships with general contractors as a primary client base rather than marketing to end consumers.

The Truth

Subcontracting to general contractors as a primary client-acquisition channel is a real, established strategy in the trades, since a single trusted GC relationship can generate far more repeat volume than one-off homeowner jobs, and permit-data prospecting is a real, used tactic. This episode is a real-time coaching conversation rather than a finished, proven system, so the scaling tactics discussed are proposed next steps, not yet demonstrated results.
