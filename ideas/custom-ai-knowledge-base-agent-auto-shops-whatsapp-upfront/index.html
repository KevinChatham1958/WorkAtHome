What It Is

You build a private chat assistant for a service business, such as an auto shop, drywall firm or real estate office, trained on its own data: quotes, margins, parts suppliers, follow-ups and staff. The creator uses Claude to code a branded dashboard in Next.js, connects AI models through OpenRouter and links the business's other tools through their public API docs. He then connects WhatsApp through a service called WATI so staff can ask the assistant questions in their group chat and get daily recaps. The build is sold for a one-time fee, after about two weeks of the client's staff testing it and reporting errors.

The Pitch

The creator says most service businesses run on scattered, outdated systems and that a single hub that answers questions and drafts quotes in seconds saves the owner hours. He claims $25,000 in a week from this agent, all paid up front with no ongoing maintenance, and says these builds can sell for $20,000 to $40,000. He argues non-technical people who can sell and build relationships have the edge in this market.

Who It’s Best For

People comfortable with AI coding tools and with selling to business owners, who can handle technical integration and testing or hire developers who can.

The Truth (as we see it)

Internal assistants built on a company's own data are a real and useful service, and the demo shows a plausible workflow. The $25,000 week and $20,000 to $40,000 prices are the creator's own unaudited claims, and he says his team of developers does the actual builds, so the 15-minute demo is not the finished product. Connecting reliably to suppliers and business systems, getting quotes accurate, and protecting client data take real skill and time. The creator runs his own AI agency (Vocal Chat) and points viewers to a free prompt dashboard linked in his bio, which may lead to further offers.

Our assessment, based on the video and what’s typical across similar videos. We don’t independently verify income claims or prices.
