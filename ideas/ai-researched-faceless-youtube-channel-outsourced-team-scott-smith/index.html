Faceless YouTube Automation Channel

Faceless YouTube automation channel with AI niche research and an outsourced $75-per-video team

Scott Smith · How I’d Build A $20k/mo YouTube Automation Business With Claude (If I Had to Start Over) ↗

What It Is

You run a faceless YouTube channel as an owner rather than a creator, earning from YouTube ad revenue once the channel passes 1,000 subscribers and 4,000 watch hours. The creator picks a niche (tech, history, military, animals, TV and movies) by checking for both evergreen and trending topics, demand and enough ideas, then builds a list of 'reference channels' using YouTube search, ViewStats and Manus AI. Claude is used to name and brand the channel, analyze competitors' recent videos, produce a ranked idea bank with titles and spot content gaps. A freelance scriptwriter, editor and thumbnail designer hired on Upwork (with an ElevenLabs AI voice) produce each video for about $75, tracked on a Trello board and guided by AI-written instruction documents (SOPs).

The Pitch

The creator says one $75 video can earn for years, showing an evergreen video that has made over $12,900 and still brings in about $1,000 a month, and a trending one that made $55,000 before dying off. He shows a channel that was monetized about three weeks after its first upload and made $13,095 in its first month, and says high-paying niches can earn $10 to $30+ per 1,000 views. His claim is that AI now cuts weeks of research down to one sitting.

Who It’s Best For

Someone with a budget to pay freelancers for many videos before seeing income, who is comfortable managing contractors and analyzing data rather than making content themselves.

The Truth (as we see it)

The step-by-step method (reference channels, competitor title analysis, outsourced production) is a common and workable approach among YouTube automation creators, and the AI prompts shown are practical. The earnings screenshots are the creator's own and unaudited, and they come from established channels; most new channels earn nothing until monetized, and many never reach that point. YouTube has tightened rules on mass-produced and reused content, so AI-heavy faceless channels carry a real risk of being denied or losing monetization. The video repeatedly pitches the creator's paid one-on-one program and his vetted content teams via a booked sales call, which is how he makes money from this video.

Our assessment, based on the video and what’s typical across similar videos. We don’t independently verify income claims or prices.
