YouTube-Fed Coaching Business

Small expert YouTube channel that turns viewers into one-to-one coaching clients (over 40)

  • Under $100 to start

What It Is

You make YouTube videos that answer the frequent questions people ask about something you know well, then invite viewers into a free 20-minute one-to-one 'discovery call' and offer paid help with the other problems you spot. The creator plans content in four 'buckets' (what you enjoy, your service, your business, your authority), three videos each, for a first three months of content, using YouTube search, Google and Answer the Public to phrase titles. Money comes from coaching, consulting or a paid program, not from ad revenue. He says his own channel of 28,000 subscribers earns about $80 a month from AdSense while his business brings in about $25,000 a month.

The Pitch

The creator argues that views are the wrong measure for people over 40: a small audience of the right people, served through helpful one-to-one conversations, converts far better than chasing viral videos. He says he sold 12 places in a $2,000 program in a week from videos getting about 400 views each, and that 40 of 41 people who booked calls bought his earlier course. He says he runs it alone, four days a week, four to six hours a day.

Who It’s Best For

People over 40 with real experience or expertise others pay for, who are comfortable on camera and enjoy one-to-one conversations with potential clients.

The Truth (as we see it)

Using a small, focused YouTube channel to attract coaching or consulting clients is a well-established approach, and the point that ad revenue matters less than trust is sound. But it depends on having expertise people will actually pay for, being on camera, and doing sales calls, even if he frames them as kind, low-pressure conversations. The income and conversion figures are the creator's own, unaudited. The video is itself a funnel for his paid Heartbrand Blueprint course and $2,000 '100 Day Accelerator' program, plus a free community that leads to them.

Our assessment, based on the video and what’s typical across similar videos. We don’t independently verify income claims or prices.
