eBay Reselling for Retirees

A paced, sell-through-rate-driven eBay reselling system for retirees

  • Online stores
  • Very low startup cost

Retired with Purpose · Retirees! 3 eBay Sales a Day Can Make Retirement Comfortable – Here's How! #13 ↗

What It Is

A structured, sustainable-pace approach to eBay reselling built around a specific target (three $20-profit sales a day, roughly $1,200 a month) and a disciplined inventory-scaling method: only buy items after checking their eBay "sell-through rate" (how many similar listings have actually sold in the last 90 days, not just how many are listed), and always source more inventory than you're currently selling so your listing count grows ahead of your sales pace.

The Pitch

The presenter, a retiree who ran a retail store for decades before moving to eBay, frames the sell-through-rate check as the single most important habit: an item with 100 listings and zero solds in 90 days is a trap regardless of how promising the listed price looks, while an item with a 100% sell-through rate at a mid-range price is a near-guaranteed sale. He documents a real 30-day challenge starting with just $2 (a camera lens bought for $2 at a thrift store, resold for $50, reinvested repeatedly) that grew to $350 in profit, a small but transparently tracked proof of the compounding-reinvestment approach at the center of the system.

Who It’s Best For

Retirees or anyone comfortable with a steady, unhurried pace of sourcing from thrift stores, estate sales, and yard sales, who wants a sustainable, list-and-replace rhythm rather than a high-intensity flipping operation.

The Truth (as we see it)

The 30-day, $2-to-$350 challenge is a specific, real, transparently walked-through example (rather than a vague income claim), a good sign, though it's one documented run, not an audited long-term average. The sell-through-rate methodology itself is genuinely sound, checkable research advice that applies to reselling on eBay generally, not specific to any product or tool the presenter is selling.
