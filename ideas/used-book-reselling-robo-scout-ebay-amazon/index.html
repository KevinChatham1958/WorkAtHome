Reselling Used Books on eBay and Amazon, Using Barcode-scanning Software to Identify Which Books Are Actually Worth Selling

E-commerce · Low (a scouting software subscription like Robo Scout; a barcode scanner once doing volume)

Originally published Sep 3, 2026 — Josh Graff

Josh Graff · Start Selling Books Online Like THIS in 2026 ↗

What It Is

Set up seller accounts on both eBay (fast, simple) and Amazon (takes weeks due to identity verification, so start early even if not selling there yet), then use book-scouting software to scan a book's barcode/ISBN and instantly see its current Amazon resale price and a 180-day 'sell score' showing how often it actually sells, filtering out the vast majority of books not worth reselling (anything under roughly $10-12 isn't worth the ~$4.50 media-mail shipping) and focusing on non-fiction, obscure, or collectible titles that sell reliably at $12+; source inventory from estate sales (best, rarely picked over) and thrift stores (more hit-or-miss, often already picked through).

The Pitch

The presenter, who sells used books full-time to support his family, walks through live examples: one book with a sell score of 79/180 days and a $23 resale price ('a no-brainer'), and another worth roughly $100 used despite being harder to find in stock, illustrating exactly how to read the scouting software's output.

Best For

Someone with access to estate sales/thrift stores in their area, willing to sort through dozens or hundreds of books to find the handful actually worth listing.

The Truth

Used book arbitrage via barcode-scanning scout apps is a real, long-established reselling niche, and the specific economics described reflect genuine, practical knowledge from someone doing this as an actual full-time business. No specific personal revenue figures are given in this excerpt, though the presenter states this is his full-time income source.
