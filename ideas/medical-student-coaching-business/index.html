Medical Student Coaching

Alex Hormozi interviewing a vascular surgery resident · You're Sitting on a $5M Business and Doing Surgery for the Same Pay ↗

What It Is

A coaching business aimed at medical students, built and run by a doctor while he was still in medical and surgical training, requiring roughly an hour a day of his time and reaching about $500,000 a year in revenue and roughly $400,000 a year in profit over five years.

The Pitch

Hormozi highlights this as a case of someone about to start a high-paying surgical career who instead already has a coaching business generating comparable income for a small fraction of the working hours, and argues the coaching business has a much higher ceiling to scale further than trading additional hours for surgical pay does.

Who It’s Best For

Someone with genuine, credentialed subject-matter expertise (in this case, having successfully navigated medical school) who is comfortable building an audience or coaching offer around that specific expertise, potentially as a side income tested during a demanding day job or training program before committing to it full-time.

The Truth (as we see it)

This is a short interview clip, not a walkthrough, so it doesn't explain what specifically is taught, how students are found and enrolled, or what's actually charged per client. The revenue, profit, and "about an hour a day" time figures are self-reported by the guest in casual conversation, not independently verified, and Hormozi is a business coach with a general commercial interest in promoting coaching-style businesses. Still, hearing this directly from a named, credentialed practitioner describing his own going concern is a stronger signal than a purely hypothetical pitch.
