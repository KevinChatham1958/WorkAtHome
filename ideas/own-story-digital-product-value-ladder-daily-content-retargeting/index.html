Digital products and tiered coaching built from your own results, sold through heavy daily posting, clippers and retargeting ads

What It Is

You package something you have done yourself (the guest's first was how he raised his credit score from 475 to over 700) into an e-book, step-by-step guide, template or course, then add higher tiers: here a $50/month membership, a $1,000-$2,000 core program and a $25,000 package. Buyers come from posting on Facebook, Instagram, TikTok and YouTube several times a day, mixing teaching, everyday life, results and client testimonials, with early clients helped free to create proof. Paid ads start small: about $20 a day to push 20 value videos to viewers, then $50-$100 a day retargeting people who watched over a minute, testing 25-50 ad versions and keeping the winners. Later he pays a community of young clippers about $3 per 1,000 views to repost short clips of his long videos on their own pages.

The Pitch

The guest, Kenny 'Smitty the Goat' Smith, says a product made once can be sold over and over, unlike trading time for money. He says he has made over $15 million online in five years and recently had a $1 million month, mostly from a joint launch with other creators, with recurring income from his membership and a credit software tool. He credits daily posting over two to three years, warming people up with free content before selling, and reaching new audiences through clippers and paid collaborations.

Who It’s Best For

People with a real result or skill others want to learn who are willing to post content every day for years and to manage ad spending carefully.

The Truth (as we see it)

This is an interview, not a tutorial, and the plan is general: the useful specifics are the price tiers, the posting rhythm and the small-budget retargeting sequence. The income figures are the guest's own and unverified, and his biggest month came from a joint launch with established names and about $275,000 in marketing, which a beginner cannot copy. He frankly admits most of his investments failed, and he sells marketing coaching, a membership, events and credit-repair software, so teaching others to sell knowledge is itself his business. Expect to build the audience slowly before ads or clippers pay off, and note that digital products in money topics like credit repair are heavily regulated and easily oversold.
