A 'find, Decode, Flip' Method for Creating Original Best-selling T-shirt Designs by Reverse-engineering Why Proven Designs Sell, Rather Than Copying Them

Physical & print products · Very low (Etsy/Amazon browsing is free; an optional paid research tool)

Originally published Sep 8, 2026 — Meg Heckman

Meg Heckman · I've Designed 10,000+ T-Shirts, Here's What Actually Sells ↗

What It Is

Instead of copying an already-selling design, use a three-step process: FIND a genuinely proven best-seller (large review history, sits in a shop's bestsellers, or a competing brand's own 'best selling' sort filter); DECODE it by separating the visual style (roughly 20% of the effect) from the underlying concept or joke (roughly 80%), going one level deeper to identify the abstract 'pattern' at work (e.g. a well-known two-sided saying paired with one animal/object that can only do one side of it); then FLIP the pattern, not the specific design, by finding entirely different concepts with the same underlying shape and applying them to a completely different animal, object, or niche.

The Pitch

The presenter demonstrates the method live on a real, currently-selling Etsy shirt (a penguin captioned 'flight was never an option'), showing a full page of copycats all splitting the same customer, then generates three completely original concepts from the decoded pattern; she ties this back to her own history-niche shirt (a Thomas Jefferson procrastination joke) that generated over $73,000 in its first year.

Best For

Someone already doing or starting print-on-demand who wants a repeatable, non-copying way to generate original design concepts.

The Truth

The core insight, that extracting and reapplying an underlying conceptual pattern produces something genuinely original while copying just creates direct competition, is sound creative and competitive strategy, and the live demonstration is concrete and verifiable. The $73,000 figure is self-reported, and the video promotes her own paid course as the next step.
