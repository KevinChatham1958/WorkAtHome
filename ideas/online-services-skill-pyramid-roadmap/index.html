The Skill Pyramid for Turning an Online Service Into $10,000 a Month

Local & professional services · Very low startup cost

Originally published Feb 27, 2025 — Mark Tilbury

Share on Facebook Link copied
Source

Mark Tilbury · How to Make Your First $10,000 As a Teen (Stupid Simple) ↗

What It Is

A general framework, not a single business, for scaling any online service (video editing, copywriting, thumbnail design, social media management, and similar) into real income, built around four elements: positioning yourself to serve businesses rather than individuals, pricing based on the value/results you deliver rather than hours worked, building visible proof of your work so clients come to you, and using reliable systems (a CRM, clear deadlines) to stand out from competitors who miss deadlines.

The Pitch

Tilbury argues businesses pay fundamentally differently than individuals do: a $2,000 problem is trivial for a business already making $50,000 a month if it solves a real pain point, so repositioning the same skill toward business clients (e.g., editing for an entrepreneur selling a product instead of a small YouTuber) unlocks meaningfully higher rates without learning a new skill. He frames charging based on results (a Facebook ads specialist billing on revenue generated, not hours) as the single biggest lever for income, since it reframes the client's mental math from cost to profit.

Best For

Someone who already has, or is building, one specific online skill and wants a framework for pricing and positioning it profitably, rather than someone looking for a brand-new idea to start from scratch.

The Truth

The core framework (serve businesses, charge for outcomes, build visible proof, be reliable) is genuinely sound and echoes advice given independently by other creators in this database. The video also includes a sponsored plug for a CRM tool (Odoo) and an affiliate link for a trading platform partway through, both tied to a financial incentive separate from the core advice, and roughly the second half of the video shifts into general personal investing (savings accounts, index funds, individual stocks, crypto) rather than business advice, which is a separate topic from the roadmap itself.
