AI Receptionist for Local Trades

AI phone receptionist service sold to local trades through people you already know

Startup cost: Low startup cost (software platform subscription such as HighLevel plus phone usage) (our estimate; the video doesn’t give figures)

What It Is

You set up an AI voice agent that answers a local business's phone 24/7, takes the caller's details and books appointments, then charge the owner a monthly fee to run it. The video's examples are trades that miss many calls, such as an HVAC company and a plumber, and the agent is built on the HighLevel platform, which the creator says takes under 10 minutes per agent. Pricing shown ranges from $297 to $1,800 a month, with one HVAC client paying $997 a month plus a $1,497 setup fee. Instead of cold calling, the creator suggests offering a demo to business owners you already pay or who come to your house (plumber, stylist, mechanic, cleaner), and adding side services like Google review automation, simple websites and Google Business Profile setup.

The Pitch

The creator says trades owners lose real money on missed calls, so they see the value quickly and pay monthly, which builds recurring income without posting on social media. She says it took her two months to land her first client, and shows community members who got clients with zero cold calling, one at $1,088 a month from four small clients and another reportedly at about $7,000 a month from five clients. She argues the warm approach (asking for an opinion on your demo rather than pitching) removes most of the fear of selling.

Who It’s Best For

People comfortable talking with small-business owners and handling light tech setup, who have a local network of tradespeople and service providers they can approach.

The Truth (as we see it)

AI receptionists for local businesses are a real and growing service, and the warm-network angle is sensible advice for people who dislike cold calling. Still, this is sales work: you have to find, convince and keep paying clients, then support them when the agent mishandles a call, and the creator's own friend got 'a lot of no's' before one yes. The income figures are the creator's and her members' own, unaudited, and screenshots are not proof of lasting revenue. The video is a funnel for her paid community and '30-day sprint' coaching, a co-hosted workshop with a seller of a $697 done-for-you automation, and the HighLevel platform, so expect upsells.

Our assessment, based on the video and what’s typical across similar videos. We don’t independently verify income claims or prices.
