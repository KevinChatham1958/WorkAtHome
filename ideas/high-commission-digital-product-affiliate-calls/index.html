Digital Product Arbitrage

Selling other people's high-ticket digital products for commission through DMs and 15-minute calls

Marketing & content · Near-zero cost (no product to build; your time for content and conversations)

Originally published Nov 3, 2026 — Richard Yu

Share on Facebook Link copied
Source

Richard Yu · $629k in 30 days selling digital products... 7 SIMPLE principles ↗

What It Is

Instead of building a product, pick an existing digital product from a marketplace that publishes its affiliate payout, favoring ones where one sale pays well (a $500 product at 70% pays $350). Work backward from an income goal: $5,000 a month at $500 a sale is 10 sales, which the creator says takes about 30 real conversations a week at his one-in-ten message close rate, or fewer if people get on a short call. Post content whose only job is to start conversations, reply to anyone who comments or messages within 15 minutes, ask what made them reach out, and offer a free 15-minute screen-share call before ever sending a link.

The Pitch

The creator argues beginners fail by starting five things at once and by building products nobody has bought; borrowing a proven product removes that risk, and few competitors will do the slow, personal work of real conversations.

Who It's Best For

People comfortable talking with strangers in DMs and on short calls who will stick with one product for months.

The Truth (as we see it)

The core advice (focus on one thing, do the math, have real conversations, prefer products that pay meaningful commissions) is sound affiliate practice. The catch is the marketplaces: many high-commission products on make-money marketplaces are themselves courses about making money, and some are resell-rights schemes, so judge the product's real value as the creator himself urges. Results like his $629,000 month and a client's $84,000 year are his own, unaudited figures. The video funnels viewers into the creator's free masterclass, the entry point to his paid programs.
