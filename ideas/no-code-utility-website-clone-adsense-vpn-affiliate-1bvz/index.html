What It Is

You build a simple single-purpose web tool, such as a checker that tells visitors whether a website is down, by describing it in plain English to an AI website builder (Hostinger AI Builder in the video). You then add an FAQ, legal pages and a few guide articles so the site can pass Google AdSense review, and earn from display ads priced per thousand page views (the creator estimates $3-7 RPM for US-heavy traffic). A second income stream is affiliate articles, for example recommending VPNs at $30-100 per sign-up when a site works for others but not for the visitor. To pick a tool that can rank, the creator uses a keyword tool to find low-difficulty searches where Google is already showing new, low-authority sites on page one.

The Pitch

The creator points to established utility sites he estimates at $10,000 to $700,000 a month and argues that tools solving one evergreen problem keep earning once built, with no inventory or customer service. He says AI builders now let non-coders make such a site in about 15 minutes, and that the hard part, getting traffic, is handled by choosing searches where new sites already rank.

Who It’s Best For

People comfortable with patient, low-cost experimenting who are willing to build several small sites and wait months for search traffic.

The Truth (as we see it)

The video is sponsored by Hostinger AI Builder and also recommends a paid keyword tool and its companion idea database, so expect links with a commercial interest. The earnings figures for other sites are traffic-based estimates, not reported revenue, and the creator does not show income from his own clone. He openly says most such sites earn little or nothing, new sites often fail their first AdSense review, and ranking takes time; his advice to build a portfolio rather than bet on one site reflects that. Cloning a well-known tool puts you up against established competitors, so the keyword-gap research is the part that matters most.

Our assessment, based on the video and what’s typical across similar videos. We don’t independently verify income claims or prices.
