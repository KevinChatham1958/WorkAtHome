Validating a Faceless YouTube Niche

Validating a faceless AI YouTube niche by studying real small channels' growth pattern, RPM, and outlier videos before committing to it

What It Is

Before committing to a faceless AI YouTube niche, study real small, currently-growing channels (typically under 100 videos and a few months to under a year old) in a candidate topic area, checking: whether a handful of 'outlier' videos (getting far more views than the channel's average) are recent, signaling the niche/format is still working right now rather than having already peaked; the channel's approximate RPM for that content category (RPM varies enormously by topic, from roughly $1 in some niches up to $8-13 in higher-paying ones like space or history); and whether the channel uses a consistent thumbnail style and a simple, repeatable production process a new creator could replicate. Once validated, post a first batch of videos (15-20 suggested) as a genuine proof-of-concept test before deciding whether to continue or pivot.

The Pitch

The presenter walks through roughly a dozen real, currently-growing small channels live, naming specific working niches (firearms/guns content, history channels, space content, 'hidden places' conspiracy-adjacent history, AI-character-driven lifestyle/beauty advice, and political commentary), citing each one's actual subscriber count, view totals, and estimated RPM.

Who It’s Best For

Someone willing to study real comparable channels' data before choosing a niche, rather than guessing, and willing to test with a real batch of videos before fully committing.

The Truth (as we see it)

The specific analytical signals described (outlier recency, RPM varying by content category, small-channel proof-of-concept batches) reflect genuine, practical YouTube-growth knowledge, and the channels/figures shown are real, current, and independently viewable on YouTube. The video promotes the presenter's own free community and an upcoming paid-adjacent workshop, so that next step is promotional, though the core research methodology doesn't depend on joining it.
