Brand-First Apparel Store Funnel

Apparel brand that treats print-on-demand as just fulfillment and builds the ad-to-email sales funnel around it

Meg Heckman · Everyone Thinks Print On Demand Is A Scam (They're Wrong) ↗

What It Is

You build an online apparel brand where print-on-demand only handles printing and shipping after the sale, and you do the selling. Using her Sloth Hiking Club brand as the example, Meg lays out the chain: Facebook and Instagram ads bring visitors; the home page quickly explains the brand, with clear, same-colored buttons and fast loading; a discount pop-up collects emails; the product page answers sizing and fit questions; then cart and checkout. After the order, emails about new designs and sales, plus retargeting ads to visitors who didn't buy, bring customers back.

The Pitch

She argues print-on-demand earned a 'scam' reputation because people were sold fulfillment (no inventory, no shipping) as if it were the whole business, uploaded designs, and waited for sales that never came. She says the money is made by everything before the order: the customer you pick, the brand angle, traffic, the website and follow-up.

Who It’s Best For

People drawn to print-on-demand who are ready to learn ads, store design and email marketing, not just upload designs.

The Truth (as we see it)

This is an honest correction to 'passive income' print-on-demand pitches: she plainly says you need traffic, samples, content and usually ads, which cost money. She shows the funnel but not costs or results, so it is a map rather than a plan. The video promotes her free 12-hour YouTube course.
