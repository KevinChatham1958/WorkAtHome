Embroidered Hat Print-on-Demand

Embroidered hat print-on-demand shop, applying a proven Etsy micro-niche framework

Physical & print products · Very low startup cost (roughly $40 to start: design tool, Etsy fee, research tool)

Originally published Jul 24, 2026 — Kimberly Mitchell

Share on Facebook Link copied
Source

Kimberly Mitchell · She Makes $220K/Year From Her COUCH (Steal Her Exact System) ↗

What It Is

Apply a proven print-on-demand framework (low cost and no inventory, designs built around emotional connection rather than cleverness, and an aggressively narrow micro-niche) to a specific, currently trending product category: embroidered baseball caps, using a print-on-demand platform (Gelato) that handles the embroidery, packaging, and shipping directly to the customer, with no design background required.

The Pitch

The underlying framework is attributed to Emily Odio-Sutton, a stay-at-home mom whose print-on-demand Etsy shop was independently reported to have brought in over $220,000 in a year, built on simple, mostly text-based designs targeting narrow niches (a "twin mom Halloween shirt" instead of a generic "mom shirt") rather than any design skill. As evidence the specific hat niche has real demand, the video cites a market research report projecting the baseball hat category to grow from $19.2 billion in 2024 to $26.7 billion by 2030, and a real, named Etsy shop (Distinct Headwear) that has generated over $2 million in revenue built entirely around one micro-niche: dog-breed dad hats.

Who It's Best For

Someone with no graphic design background who's willing to create many design variations, post consistently, and let real sales data (not personal taste) decide which designs to keep making.

The Truth (as we see it)

The Emily Odio-Sutton case study is independently reported by CNBC Make It, which reviewed her actual sales documents, a real, checkable source rather than a secondhand claim, though her original business sold t-shirts and candles, not hats, this video applies her general framework to a new product category rather than repeating her exact results. Worth knowing: the presenter uses, and links to, a specific print-on-demand platform (Gelato) with a referral incentive, though the underlying niche-selection and design-testing framework doesn't require that specific platform to apply.
