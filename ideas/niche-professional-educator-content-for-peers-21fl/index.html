Content for Your Own Profession

Niche educator content for people in your own profession (buyer-focused content strategy)

Content & personal brand · Near-zero cost (social media account; your own professional expertise)

Originally published Sep 25, 2026 — Alex Hormozi

Share on Facebook Link copied
Source

Alex Hormozi · How to become rich with social media (my exact playbook) ↗

What It Is

You create social media content aimed narrowly at people in your own profession about a specific money problem they have, rather than broad content for a large audience. The creator's example is a registered dietitian with under 6,000 Instagram followers who posted only about how dietitians can bill insurance, and who he says made over $1 million a year from it. He advises studying your best-paying customers, making content about their specific problems, and tracking which videos actually lead to sales using tagged links (UTMs) in descriptions and calls to action toward a lead magnet.

The Pitch

The creator says his own tests showed that broad, beginner-friendly videos broke view records but made zero sales, while his lower-view, advanced business videos made the most revenue. He argues algorithms reward what most people like, not what the most valuable buyers like, so a tiny but well-targeted audience can out-earn a large one.

Best For

Experienced professionals with specialist know-how that peers in their field would pay to learn.

The Truth

The video is a content strategy talk for people who already have something to sell; the dietitian example is a single anecdote and does not say what product or service she sold. The creator operates at a scale (billions of impressions, a large company and a book launch) that most viewers cannot replicate, and the video promotes his free roadmap lead magnet. The underlying idea, small but highly targeted audiences of professionals, is credible, but it relies on having real expertise and a paid offer, which the video does not cover.
