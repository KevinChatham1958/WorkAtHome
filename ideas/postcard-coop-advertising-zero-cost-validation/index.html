Shared Postcard Advertising

Shared group postcard advertising business, validated for free before any spending

Marketing & content · Free to validate (a free sample-postcard app to show prospects); ongoing costs (printing, postage, design tools) only begin once businesses commit)

Originally published Jul 7, 2026 — Ron Schrader

Share on Facebook Link copied
Source

Ron Schrader · Start This Side Hustle Without Spending a Penny ↗

What It Is

Sell shared ad space on a single large (9x12) group postcard mailed to a local area, where multiple businesses split the cost of one mailing by each buying a spot — validated before spending any money by showing prospective advertisers a free sample-postcard mockup app and simply asking if they'd be interested in a spot, only building the real card and collecting payment once enough businesses have said yes.

The Pitch

The recommended approach is face-to-face outreach (not cold social media/email) starting with businesses you already know or are comfortable approaching, using early "yes" commitments as social proof to help land subsequent businesses ("I've got so-and-so on this postcard, would you like to join them?"), and optionally giving one prominent business a free first spot to kick-start momentum — with a $7/month membership (templates, design help, training) offered only once there's already validated interest, not as a prerequisite to start.

Who It's Best For

Someone willing to have short, low-pressure, in-person conversations with local business owners, comfortable with a genuinely zero-cost validation-before-building approach rather than needing to commit money upfront.

The Truth (as we see it)

Co-op/shared direct mail advertising is a long-established, legitimate local marketing model (splitting one mailing's cost across several advertisers), and validating demand before spending anything is sound, low-risk business practice. The presenter is candid that this requires real face-to-face selling effort (not a passive listing), and that some prospects who say "not now" may still come around later if you keep the option open.
