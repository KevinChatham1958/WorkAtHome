What It Is

Before you start a channel, decide how it will earn money in the medium to long term: bringing clients to a business, selling coaching, courses or a mastermind, feeding a subscription or software product, or Patreon. Copy formats that already work in your niche, but add your own personality and side interests so viewers know you for something distinct. You then post consistently and use the channel to send viewers to whatever you sell. The channel itself may earn ad revenue too, but the main money comes from the product or service behind it.

The Pitch

Gadzhi calls a YouTube channel 'one of the biggest blessings' anyone can have: results take 3 to 6 months or longer, but they come quickly once they arrive, especially with a monetization plan in place. He says you don't need to reinvent the wheel, only add your own personality to formats that already work.

Who It’s Best For

Patient people with something to sell or teach who are comfortable on camera.

The Truth (as we see it)

Sound advice (plan the money first rather than relying on ads), but the video gives no numbers or step-by-step plan, and Gadzhi's own success sits on years of weekly uploads. Most channels take far longer than 3 to 6 months to pay. This video was published in 2022, more than two years ago, so tools, platform rules, rates and income figures may have changed.

Our assessment, based on the video and what’s typical across similar videos. We don’t independently verify income claims or prices.

What Viewers Say

Mostly encouragement and thanks. The practical comments come from video editors, who say finding clients who will trust a newcomer, especially from another country, is much harder than the video makes it sound.
