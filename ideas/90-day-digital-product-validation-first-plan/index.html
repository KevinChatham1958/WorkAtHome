90-Day Digital Product Plan

A 90-day plan for launching a digital product alongside a full-time job: build an audience first, pre-sell to validate before creating anything, then deliver and iterate

  • Digital products
  • Under $100 to start
  • Jump to The Truth ↓

From Sandra Di

Startup cost: Very low (a free platform account, a basic checkout page tool)

Source video

Sandra Di · If You Have a Job, Start Selling Digital Products ↗

What It Is

Follow a structured 90-day sequence rather than building a product first: in the first 30 days, pick a niche at the intersection of passion, real experience, and market demand, choose one platform to show up on consistently (YouTube recommended for search-driven discovery), and offer a free lead magnet closely related to your eventual paid offer to build an email list, since most sales come from that list, not social followers. In the next 30 days, validate instead of guessing: survey your list or run casual DM/Zoom conversations, outline a simple product based on that feedback, then launch it to 'founding members' at a discount through a simple checkout page before building anything in full; five sales is treated as proof the offer is worth completing. In the final 30 days, build and deliver the product, collect feedback and testimonials, and resist the urge to immediately start a new product — instead keep promoting and improving that same validated offer while continuing to grow the email list.

The Pitch

The presenter, a former full-time engineer, started selling digital products in 2020 while still employed, describes a genuinely slow start, and reports that by 2022 her side income had surpassed her engineering salary; she's explicit that building the product before validating demand 'literally cost me years' of her own time when she made it herself.

Who It’s Best For

Someone currently employed full-time who wants to build a digital product business in evening hours without quitting first, willing to validate demand before investing real time in building anything.

The Truth (as we see it)

The core sequencing reflects sound, widely-taught direct-response and lean-startup principles rather than vague hype, and the presenter's own candid account of a slow start and a real personal mistake adds credibility. Her specific income transition timeline is self-reported and not independently verified, and the video promotes her own free lead-magnet guide as a next step.
