Dog Treat Bakery

Dog treat baking business selling to pet boutiques and farmers markets

Physical & print products · Very low startup cost (packaging and labels run 30-80 cents/unit; uses equipment most kitchens already have)

What It Is

Bake dog treats (the presenter's example: peanut butter biscuits) using standard home baking equipment, and sell them to pet boutiques and at weekly farmers markets, while navigating that dog treats are regulated as pet food in many states rather than falling under simpler cottage food laws.

The Pitch

A dozen peanut butter biscuits cost $1.15-2.05 all-in and sell for $8-12/dozen standard, $10-16 decorated, with gift boxes going $18-35 — about $8.40 profit per dozen at a $10 price point. Six dozen at a market nets around $50; 10-15 dozen nets $85-125. The presenter flags a regulatory detail almost nobody checks: dog treats are classified as pet food, not covered by cottage food law in many states, and some require a commercial feed license with AAFCO nutrient testing even for home-kitchen production.

Who It's Best For

Someone with basic baking equipment already at home who's willing to research their specific state's pet food regulations before their first sale, and who wants pet boutiques and market regulars as repeat customers.

The Truth (as we see it)

The regulatory warning is the single most important piece of information here — treating this like a standard cottage food business without checking state pet-food rules first is a real, easy-to-miss legal risk, not just a formality.
