3-2-1 Short-Form Content Strategy

The '3-2-1' short-form content strategy: posting a mix of broad and narrow topic buckets, guided by Claude-analyzed performance data instead of guessing

  • Low

What It Is

Structure a weekly short-form content posting schedule (originally 3-2-1 = 3 broad-topic videos, 2 medium, 1 narrow) using a mental model of concentric 'rings' from broad (general business/money topics, huge reach but low buyer-fit) down to narrow (your exact ideal customer's specific problem, smaller reach but far higher conversion); use a Claude Skill that interviews you (who specifically benefits most from your content, their one core struggle) to generate a personalized visual map of these rings. Feed your own posted videos' transcripts and performance data into Claude to identify, per topic/hook/format category, which specific buckets are statistically outperforming your average, then post more in winning buckets and cut underperformers in your next batch. Explicitly separate two different content goals requiring opposite strategies: broad, viral-optimized content for brand-deal revenue, versus narrower, precisely-targeted content for converting a specific buyer into a customer or lead.

The Pitch

The presenter (a guest, Callaway) reports going from a smaller following to 2 million combined subscribers across his channels over three years, with his specific 'Callaway Marketing' account reaching 60,000 followers and six figures in attributable revenue in 100 days by deliberately NOT trying to go viral, instead keeping his audience precisely aligned with buyers; he demonstrates the Claude Skill live, including a real example where analyzing 11 similar videos pinpointed the specific word difference between his best and worst performers.

Who It’s Best For

Someone already posting short-form content (or about to start) who wants a data-driven, testable system for choosing topics rather than guessing, comfortable using Claude Skills/connected analytics tools.

The Truth (as we see it)

The core insight, that broad viral content and narrow conversion-focused content are genuinely different games requiring different targeting, is sound, non-obvious content-marketing logic backed by a real, live-demonstrated example. The presenter's own follower/revenue figures are self-reported, and the specific Claude Skill and analytics tool demonstrated are the guest's own paid products, so those tool recommendations are promotional even though the underlying framework doesn't require them.
