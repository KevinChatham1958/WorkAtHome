AI Ebook Multi-Channel Sales

AI-written ebook built from Amazon review complaints, sold on six channels (Medium, Google Books, Kindle, Gumroad, own page, free-plus-shipping funnel)

What It Is

You have an AI tool pull the top 10 bestsellers in a category from Amazon with their rank, price and page count, then list the three most-praised and three most-complained-about points in buyers' own words; complaints that show up across several books become your chapters. AI then plans the book backwards from the reader's end goal and writes it chapter by chapter (about 2,000 words each in his prompts). He ranks six places to sell it from worst to best: Medium's partner program (about 2 cents a view), Google Books (70% royalty), Amazon Kindle (70% royalty up to $12.99, with an offer placed inside the book), Gumroad (about 10% fee, optional paid 'boost'), your own sales page (best for higher-priced books aimed at people who earn money, like a $59 guide for photographers), and a 'free plus shipping' page with an audiobook upsell, a course upsell and a cheaper downsell. He promotes the book with a faceless YouTube video covering its top 10 ideas.

The Pitch

The creator says researching real buyer complaints is why the book sells when most self-published books don't, and that selling to people who already earn money removes price resistance. He cites Molly ($500,000 from a 13-page $59 booklet), Brian Moran ($1.2 million from short ebooks), and the free-plus-shipping model used by Russell Brunson and Alex Hormozi. He suggests listing on all six channels at once, then translating and narrating successful books for other languages.

Who It’s Best For

People who are good at a subject or can judge AI-drafted content, and who are willing to edit carefully and build a simple sales page and promotion over time.

The Truth (as we see it)

Selling short, problem-solving ebooks on Kindle, Gumroad and your own page is a real, established model, and the review-complaint research step is a sensible way to pick content. The big success stories (Molly, Brian Moran, Hormozi's sales figures) are other people's results quoted by the creator, not his own, and his example sales numbers are illustrations, not shown earnings. The free-plus-shipping funnel needs printed books, printing and shipping fulfillment and real upsell products, none of which the video explains; Kindle also asks publishers to disclose AI-generated content, and lightly edited AI books risk poor reviews. Despite the 'from your phone' title, the demo uses desktop Claude Code. The video is sponsored by Mythos AI (an app builder with a free trial) and pushes a free text guide of prompts.

Our assessment, based on the video and what’s typical across similar videos. We don’t independently verify income claims or prices.
