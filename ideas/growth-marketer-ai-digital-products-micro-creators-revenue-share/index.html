What It Is

You find small creators (roughly 10,000 to 100,000 followers in a tight niche, such as fitness coaches, language teachers or gardening experts) who have an audience but nothing to sell. Instead of a generic pitch, you send each one a 'monetization game plan': an AI audit of their content showing what their audience responds to, product ideas, a suggested price, projected revenue and a launch plan. When a creator agrees, you use AI to build the product (a coaching offer, ebook or video course with modules and worksheets), then hand the creator a 14-day Instagram story script: three days of warm-up, six of free value, then five days with the cart open. The creator posts, sales run through Whop, and you keep a share, suggested at 30% to start with 70% for the creator.

The Pitch

Gadzhi calls this the most beginner-friendly model he has seen: one deal can pay $2,000 to $50,000, there is no ad spend or inventory, and you never show your face. He says a creator with 50,000 to 100,000 followers typically makes 100 to 250 sales at $100 to $200 on a first launch, so a 30% cut is $3,000 to $15,000, and that relaunching monthly with one or two new creators a month builds recurring income.

Who It’s Best For

People comfortable doing persistent personalized outreach and managing a creator relationship, who want to work behind the scenes rather than build their own audience.

The Truth (as we see it)

This is a long sales video for UpLevel Consulting at consulting.com, Gadzhi's own paid program, and the 'just click the buttons' tools shown (Synthesize AI and Ghostwriter OS) come with it. He is also a co-owner of Whop, which takes a cut of the sales. The student results and the typical-launch figures are unaudited claims from his own clients, and he admits his own results are not typical. The model itself (helping a creator package and launch a product for a revenue split) is real, but the hard parts are getting a creator to say yes, making a product good enough to carry their name, and getting paid what you agreed. Ordinary AI tools can do most of the drafting without buying a program.

Our assessment, based on the video and what’s typical across similar videos. We don’t independently verify income claims or prices.

What Viewers Say

The most common complaint is cost: viewers say the AI tools shown run to thousands of dollars, which clashes with the claim that you can start with almost nothing, and one joke points at the creator's paid coaching program pinned under the video. Others say the hard part is landing clients, not making the product, and the most-liked comment jokes that the surest money is in making videos like this. The rest is thanks and holiday greetings.
