"Adaptation Over Cloning" Niche SaaS Repositioning Built With AI

Digital & subscription products · Low startup cost (an AI coding tool like Claude, plus optional developer help for finishing touches)

Originally published Sep 11, 2026 — affiliatemarketingmc

Share on Facebook Link copied
Source

affiliatemarketingmc — https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mdmphrHkeC8

What It Is

Instead of cloning an existing app outright (which raises legal and competitive problems), pick an established, revenue-proven SaaS category with known user frustrations (the video's example: generic support-focused live chat software), identify a specific underserved buyer or use case within that category the incumbents don't optimize for (e.g. sales-focused live chat instead of support-focused live chat), and use AI coding tools to quickly build a repositioned MVP around that one specific outcome, then sell it both as a low-cost productized subscription and as a higher-priced custom/done-for-you implementation.

The Pitch

The presenter built a live-chat tool ("SellerPole") in about 48 hours using Claude for most of the coding plus a developer for finishing details, explicitly reframing the whole live-chat product around helping salespeople act on buying intent before a visitor leaves (built-in sales pipeline notes, mass-messaging site visitors) rather than the industry-standard support-ticket framing — arguing that researching competitor complaints (via tools like Ahrefs or searching "[product] alternatives") and choosing one specific underserved buyer is what makes an adapted SaaS product sellable, not the software itself.

Best For

Someone comfortable using AI coding tools to build a working MVP quickly, and willing to do real market research (studying complaints about existing tools in a category) before building, rather than building speculatively.

The Truth

The core lesson — that repositioning a proven, revenue-validated software category around an underserved specific buyer beats cloning a feature set — is sound product strategy used by many real, larger businesses (the video cites Tesla vs. Prius and Celsius vs. energy drinks as analogies). The presenter is explicit that "cloning" language is marketing framing, not literal copying, and that results (including his own) are not typical or guaranteed; the video also promotes his own paid mentorship community, a financial incentive worth factoring in.
