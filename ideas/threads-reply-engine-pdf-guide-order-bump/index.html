Threads PDF Guide

Complaint-based PDF guide sold through Threads conversation posts with a checkout add-on

Digital & subscription products · Near-zero cost (AI subscription, checkout platform fees, short domain)

Originally published Aug 26, 2026 — Frank Garrett

Share on Facebook Link copied
Source

Frank Garrett · I just used Threads + PDFs for an $18,970 in 7 Days business ↗

What It Is

You spend about two hours searching a topic on Threads and its communities, copying the exact complaints people repeat in the replies to popular posts. You paste those complaints into Claude, have it ask you five questions, then build a short practical guide chapter by chapter, run a critique pass and read it all yourself; you also make a small set of fill-in templates. The guide goes on a checkout platform at $37 with the templates as a $19 add-on (order bump), linked from a short custom domain to a single sales page. Growth comes from posts that tell a small true story and end with a question, thoughtful replies under big accounts' fresh posts, answering every reply in the first hour, and putting the link in the first reply under your own post.

The Pitch

The creator says he made $18,970 in seven days: 410 sales of the $37 guide plus 200 buyers adding the $19 templates. He says Threads has over 400 million monthly users but less creator competition than Instagram or TikTok, so an account with a few hundred followers can reach thousands, and posts are just text with no filming. He argues the add-on box is the most underrated part, bringing in about 20% of revenue for minutes of setup.

Best For

Good writers who can't or won't appear on camera and are willing to spend daily time replying and starting conversations on social media.

The Truth

The tactics are concrete and sensible: build from real complaints, use an order bump, keep links clean and focus on replies. The $18,970 week from an account with only a few thousand followers is his own unaudited claim and is far above what most beginners should expect. Threads' reach and link rules change, and he admits he didn't collect emails, so everything depended on a platform he doesn't control. A guide that tells people how to sell digital products is a crowded, somewhat self-referential niche. He promotes his own paid course, the Passive PDF Method.
