AI Support Agent Setup Service

Setting up AI customer support agents for businesses for a setup fee plus monthly retainer

  • AI & automation
  • Low startup cost

Chris Koerner on The Koerner Office Podcast · The Easiest Way to Make Money With AI Right Now ↗

What It Is

You build an AI agent that answers a business's customer questions from its own help docs, product manuals and website, then install it as a website chat (or phone) assistant that hands off to a human when stuck. The guest, who runs the chatbot platform DocsBot, says his agency partners typically charge around $2,000-3,000 to set it up and $500-1,000 a month to maintain it, while paying the platform roughly $50-150 a month per agent. Prospects are found by having an AI assistant search for businesses with a knowledge base but no chatbot, or companies currently hiring support staff, then sending them a working demo bot built on their own docs. The monthly fee covers an AI-generated report showing tickets handled and gaps in the client's help documentation.

The Pitch

The guest says small businesses lack the time and expertise to set up AI themselves, so they need someone to implement it, and the value is easy to prove: if a bot answers half of 2,000 monthly tickets that each take five minutes, the client saves several thousand dollars a month against a $500 fee. He says the work is now mostly automatable for non-technical people, and that recurring retainers stack up. He reports his own software business passed $1 million in annual recurring revenue with about 90% gross margins.

Who It’s Best For

Someone comfortable pitching businesses by phone, text or in person and explaining return on investment, who is willing to learn a chatbot platform well enough to maintain clients' bots.

The Truth (as we see it)

The guest is the founder of DocsBot and runs a partner program for agencies, so the recommendation to resell his platform is self-interested; other chatbot platforms exist. The $2,000 setup and $500/month figures are what he says he hears from agency partners, not audited numbers, and his own $1 million figure is software revenue, not service revenue. He is candid that cold email and LinkedIn messages are increasingly ineffective and that in-person meetings and phone calls work best, so this is a sales-driven business. His 19-year-old son's first contract is described, but its price and results were not yet known at recording.
