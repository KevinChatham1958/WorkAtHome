Front-Porch Micro Bakery

Front-porch micro bakery selling home-baked bread and pastries on Saturday mornings with online pre-orders

What It Is

You bake from your home kitchen under your state's cottage food law and sell from a cart or folding table at your own front door once a week. The featured baker bakes most items on Friday (sourdough, cookies, muffins) and cinnamon rolls and scones Saturday morning, takes pre-orders and card payments through Hotplate, and accepts Venmo, Zelle and cash at the cart. Prices run about $12 for sourdough, $5 for a quarter-pound cookie, $9 for four bagels and $8 for a cake slice; she reports $2,000-2,100 in Saturday sales against about $500 in ingredients and packaging, and roughly 60 hours of work per week including prep, labeling and admin. Customers find her through Nextdoor, local Facebook groups, word of mouth and TikTok videos filmed while building and running the stand; she also sells sourdough starter for $5.

The Pitch

The creator says a 20-year-old living with her parents built a stand that sells out within an hour most Saturdays, with lines of 80-100 people and no paid marketing, after one 20-second TikTok went viral locally. He says you do not need a big audience, a busy road or a large neighborhood, and that a weekly staple like bread brings repeat buyers. The baker recommends perfecting one product, giving samples to family and friends for feedback, and adding pre-orders early so sales are set even if it rains.

Who It’s Best For

Someone who already bakes well, lives where neighbors can easily stop by, and can handle a long weekly baking and packaging routine.

The Truth (as we see it)

The numbers are the baker's own, unaudited, and she says the viral moment and current social media interest are big factors that may not last; she also had a small customer base from years of custom cakes. It is real physical work, about 60 hours a week by her estimate, so the hourly return is far lower than the "$2,000 in one hour" framing suggests. Cottage food rules vary widely by state and country: some limit what you can sell, require labels, inspections or a food handler or manager card, and Texas does not allow an unattended stand. Neighborhood rules on home sales and customer traffic are worth checking before you start.
