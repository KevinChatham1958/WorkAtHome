Dried Fruit Production

Dried fruit production business using a small dehydrator

Physical & print products · Low startup cost (16-tray stainless dehydrators around $95-135; larger commercial cabinets cost more)

What It Is

Producing dried fruit using a small stainless dehydrator, sold as retail snack bags or supplied to food-service buyers like bars and bakeries that use dried fruit as an ingredient.

The Pitch

Small bags of dried fruit like mango commonly retail around $4-6; selling 100 bags at $5 is about $500 in gross sales before fruit, packaging, electricity, labor, retailer margin, and waste. A 16-tray stainless dehydrator runs $95-135. The presenter flags that fresh fruit loses most of its weight as water evaporates, so the real cost driver is the price per finished pound after drying, not the price of the fresh fruit going in.

Best For

Someone willing to calculate true cost-per-finished-pound before scaling, and to pursue recurring food-service buyers (a cocktail bar needing dried citrus weekly, a bakery wanting strawberry pieces) rather than relying only on retail snack-bag sales.

The Truth

The weight loss from dehydration is easy to underestimate and can make a cheap input fruit look expensive once you account for what a finished, sellable pound actually costs to produce.
