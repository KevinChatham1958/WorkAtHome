Mini-Course From Audience Questions

Building a mini-course by listening for your audience's repeated "how do you do that?" questions

Content & personal brand · Very low startup cost (a course-hosting platform and basic video editing)

Originally published Aug 31, 2026 — Brian O'Neill

Share on Facebook Link copied

What It Is

Build an audience by posting content about whatever genuinely interests you, then pay close attention to what your audience repeatedly asks you how to do — that repeated question is the signal for a specific, narrow, low-cost mini-course (not a comprehensive multi-hour course) that directly answers it, launched with visible urgency (a closing cart date) and built-in social proof from documenting the process publicly as it happens.

The Pitch

The presenter breaks down creator Jessica Carter, who grew from 50 to over 162,000 Instagram followers after pivoting to content about video editing techniques (after years of unrelated personal content), then launched a narrow $57 mini-course teaching just that one editing skill when her audience kept asking how she did it — generating $50,000 in the first 24 hours and $313,000 over 6 days, driven largely by urgency (a limited window to buy) and her own public documentation of the launch happening in real time.

Who It's Best For

Someone who's already built (or is building) an engaged audience around a specific skill or interest, and is willing to actually notice and act on repeated audience questions rather than guessing at what to sell.

The Truth (as we see it)

The core lesson — audience questions are buying signals, and a narrow mini-course beats an over-built comprehensive course — is a genuinely sound and often-overlooked product strategy. That said, this case study describes an outcome after 10 years of posting and a viral video that brought in most of the new followers; the launch mechanics (urgency, real-time documentation) are replicable, but they depend on already having an audience willing to buy, which is the harder and slower part not covered in depth here.
