Faceless YouTube Niche Scouting

Faceless YouTube niche-scouting method using revenue estimators and outlier-chasing

Content & personal brand · Very low startup cost (AI video tools like Solo AI or We Rush; some free, others low monthly cost)

Originally published Aug 24, 2026 — One Person Business

Share on Facebook Link copied
Source

One Person Business · 20 NEW Faceless Niches Paying More Than A 9-5 ↗

What It Is

Find profitable faceless YouTube niches by studying real channels' estimated revenue (using a tool called Solar Revenue Estimator) and video performance, testing a niche cheaply first (roughly $30/video for 9 test videos), then doubling down on quality and hiring once a format proves it works, watching specifically for single outlier videos that dramatically outperform a channel's other content as the strongest signal of what to replicate.

The Pitch

The presenter walks through roughly 20 real channels across niches — AI-generated horror/mystery content, deadly-animal escalation formats, long-form 25-40 minute videos aimed at Americans over 40 for higher ad rates, non-English-language channels (Spanish, Portuguese, Russian, German) facing less competition, and history/wildlife content — citing specific estimated monthly earnings ($6,000 to $94,000+) and noting several channels that make significant money despite not having posted in months, or from a single viral upload.

Who It's Best For

Someone comfortable with AI video-generation tools and consistent testing, willing to treat content niches as a data-driven experiment (cheap tests first, scale what works) rather than picking a topic based on personal interest alone.

The Truth (as we see it)

The specific tactical advice here — test cheap before investing in quality, watch for outlier videos as the real signal, and consider non-English markets for less competition — is more concrete than a lot of faceless-YouTube content, and it's a genuinely different lens on niche research than simply copying a currently-trending format. That said, the revenue figures come from a third-party estimator tool, not verified creator statements, so treat them as rough estimates; and several examples (a channel using a fake CNN logo, AI-generated news anchors) are described even by the presenter as likely to get demonetized for policy violations, which is worth taking as a real caution, not just a curiosity.
