WordPress Lead Microsite With Claude Code

14-step WordPress lead-gen microsite build with Claude Code agents (niche scoring, EMD, competitor recon)

AI & automation · Low to moderate startup cost (managed WordPress hosting such as Kinsta or Hostinger, an under-$30 domain, AI model usage)

Originally published Jul 27, 2026 — Jesse Cunningham

Share on Facebook Link copied
Source

Jesse Cunningham · How I Built 50 Microsites That Took Over a State ↗

What It Is

A full live build of a lead-generation microsite that the creator resells leads from, 'like HomeAdvisor and Angie's List'. Steps include: asking ChatGPT for profitable sub-niches, scoring niche-plus-city pairs with his tool (several options scored 41-60 out of 100 before he settled on Houston slab leak), checking Google's free Keyword Planner, bulk-checking exact-match domains in Namecheap's 'Beast Mode', installing WordPress with a custom child theme, and giving Claude Code SFTP access plus a private GitHub repo for rollbacks. Claude then sends parallel agents to profile nine competitor sites across three towns, proposes site structures, writes pages under 'house rules' (no fake reviews or invented team), redesigns from example sites, adds a WPForms contact form, expands to more suburbs, and adds schema markup. He notes the same workflow can be used to build custom websites for clients.

The Pitch

The creator says the setup takes effort the first time, but once the project 'brain' (an LLM wiki) exists, each additional site goes much faster. He shows a call from one of his sites lasting nearly nine minutes as evidence of qualified leads, and says Claude acts as a designer, developer and SEO researcher you can talk to directly. He calls this the most fun method but says cheaper methods exist for scaling to 10-30 sites.

Who It's Best For

Patient beginners who want a detailed, step-by-step walkthrough of building one quality local site with AI, either to sell leads or to sell custom websites to local businesses.

The Truth (as we see it)

The workflow is concrete and shows real hiccups, such as an ugly first design and the AI wrongly insisting it needed extra login details, which is an honest picture of working with AI agents. It relies on a stack of paid services (managed hosting, a premium AI model he admits is expensive), so costs exceed a typical low-cost side hustle, especially per site. Building a site doesn't guarantee rankings or a contractor willing to pay for leads, and the video doesn't cover selling the leads. He says the prompts used are kept inside his paid academy, and that the academy's main method differs from what is shown. Jesse Cunningham sells membership in his own Rank Expand Academy (a paid online community with a 7-day free trial, Microsite Playbook and Microsite Empire modules, weekly calls and a one-click microsite builder) and promotes it throughout the video, along with his niche-scoring tool at tool.rankexpand.com.
