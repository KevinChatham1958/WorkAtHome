Stick-Figure Explainer Videos

A free-AI-tools production pipeline for viral stick-figure explainer videos

What It Is

A specific, entirely free step-by-step process for producing simple animated "stick figure" explainer videos in a popular viral format: use Claude to analyze a successful channel's titles and generate new topic ideas in the same style, write a narration script, generate an AI voiceover, run the voiceover through a free transcription tool to extract natural pause timestamps, then feed those timestamps back into Claude to generate matching image prompts for each pause (in a consistent hand-drawn stick-figure style), and assemble the finished images and audio into a video.

The Pitch

The presenter demonstrates the entire pipeline live, producing a complete video in under 30 minutes using no paid tools, citing a real example channel that reportedly grew to roughly 15 million views and an estimated $13,000 a month within two months using this exact simple visual style. The specific timestamp-syncing technique, using a transcription tool to find natural speech pauses and building the image sequence around those exact moments rather than guessing at pacing, is presented as the key technical detail most people attempting this format get wrong.

Who It’s Best For

Someone comfortable directing multiple free AI tools together in sequence (voice generation, transcription, image generation) and willing to iterate on scripts and visual prompts to match a specific successful format.

The Truth (as we see it)

The presenter directly and specifically addresses the real risk of AI content getting demonetized or removed, correctly noting that YouTube's enforcement has mainly targeted mass-produced, repetitive, near-identical AI content rather than well-produced original videos that happen to use AI voiceover or AI-generated images, a genuinely useful and accurate clarification rather than vague reassurance. The $13,000-a-month figure for the example channel is attributed to a third-party estimation tool, not the channel's own confirmed earnings, so it should be treated as a plausible estimate rather than verified income.
