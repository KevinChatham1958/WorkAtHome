Rank-and-Rent Lead Sites

Website landlord: rank-and-rent local lead generation sites

Startup cost: Very low startup cost (~$25/month per site in tools)

Chris Koerner · The Most Passive $104K/M Online Business You've Never Heard Of ↗

What It Is

Build simple, single-niche, single-city websites (junk removal, gutters, and similar local home-service categories) optimized to rank on the first page of Google, then once the site is generating real calls, rent that lead flow to a local business owner for a flat monthly fee rather than selling the leads individually.

The Pitch

Kyle, who says he built this into a roughly $104,000-a-month business working an hour or two a day, walks through the exact closing tactic: build and rank the site first, let calls go to voicemail until you have proof of demand, then offer a business owner a free week of calls with no contract and no card on file. Because the business owner is already seeing paying customers before any money changes hands, Kyle frames the negotiation as landlord-and-tenant rather than a cold sales pitch, and says he's abandoned pay-per-call and tiered pricing in favor of one flat, non-negotiable monthly rate specifically to avoid clients auditing individual leads.

Who It’s Best For

Someone comfortable with basic SEO and content work who's willing to pick genuinely underserved local niches and cities (Kyle specifically recommends avoiding highly competitive niches like personal injury law) rather than chasing broad, contested keywords.

The Truth (as we see it)

Kyle is unusually candid about the tradeoffs of his own pricing evolution: he moved away from a pay-per-call model that was probably more profitable specifically because flat-fee billing meant less client hand-holding, a real and specific business decision rather than a marketing claim. This is a pattern that shows up repeatedly across WAH creator content, and it's worth comparing a few different practitioners' specific tactics (niche selection, pricing structure, closing scripts) before picking one approach, since the details vary in specific ways between versions of this pitch.
