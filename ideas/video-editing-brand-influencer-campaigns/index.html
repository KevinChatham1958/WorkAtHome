Video Editing for Brands

Video editing for brand and influencer campaigns

What It Is

Offer video editing services, starting with small jobs for individual creators and small businesses, then working up to editing paid brand and influencer campaign content, positioning the skill as a scalable freelance business rather than a one-off gig.

The Pitch

Tilbury cites Tom La Trousse, who taught himself editing from his university dorm room and built to $100,000 in yearly profit editing influencer collaborations for brands like Coca-Cola, Nintendo, and Gymshark. The pitch leans on video's dominance of internet traffic (cited at 82.5% of global internet traffic) as evidence of durable, growing demand, and directly addresses the AI-replacement question: the argument is that AI tools can automate mechanical cutting and beat-syncing, but can't judge emotional pacing or build genuinely original approaches the way a human editor does, so editing remains a real, in-demand skill even as AI tools mature around it.

Who It’s Best For

Someone willing to start with smaller, lower-paying jobs (TikToks and Reels for small creators) to build a portfolio before pursuing higher-value brand work, rather than expecting to land major clients immediately.

The Truth (as we see it)

The 82.5%-of-internet-traffic figure is a commonly cited industry statistic, though its original source isn't named in the video, so it's worth treating as a general industry estimate rather than an audited number. The Tom La Trousse case study is specific (named brands, a dollar figure) but comes from the presenter's own paid course content, where Tom is a featured instructor, a real financial incentive behind how the result is framed. The advice to build a portfolio through smaller jobs before pursuing brand-level clients is sound, general freelancing guidance independent of any specific tool or course.

Our assessment, based on the video and what’s typical across similar videos. We don’t independently verify income claims or prices.
