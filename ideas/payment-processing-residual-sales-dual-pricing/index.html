Payment Processing Sales

Selling merchant/payment processing with dual pricing for residual income

  • Services from home
  • Under $100 to start
  • Jump to The Truth ↓

Video published Aug 6, 2026 by Seve Ortale

Startup cost: Very low startup cost (our estimate; the video doesn’t give figures)

Share on Facebook Link copied

Source video

Seve Ortale · The New Era Of Business Is Here (And You're Not Ready) ↗

What It Is

Sign local businesses up for credit and debit card processing services, earning a small residual percentage (roughly 1%) of every transaction that business processes, for as long as they remain a customer, an ongoing income stream rather than a one-time commission. The specific pitch used to win accounts is "dual pricing," where the card processing fee is passed on to the customer paying by card (who still keeps their rewards points) instead of being absorbed by the business, effectively reducing the business owner's processing costs to near zero.

The Pitch

The core argument is that this is a genuinely durable, low-competition category precisely because it isn't exciting: card transactions in the US alone exceed $10 trillion a year, and dual pricing is described as a still-early, rapidly growing trend most business owners haven't been pitched on yet, giving an early mover real room to build a client base before it becomes commoditized. A worked example is given: a restaurant processing $60,000 a month in cards was paying $2,000 a month (3.2%) in fees; switching to dual pricing eliminated that cost for the business while the agent earns roughly 1% of that business's monthly processing volume, ongoing, for as long as the account stays active.

Who It’s Best For

Someone comfortable with in-person or phone-based B2B sales and cold approaches (the presenter describes starting by knocking on 600 business doors before landing a first deal), who's willing to build a client base slowly in exchange for compounding, long-term residual income rather than a fast payout.

The Truth (as we see it)

The presenter directly and specifically addresses the pyramid-scheme concern this business structure sometimes raises, explaining that compensation comes only from selling processing services to businesses, not from recruiting other sellers, which is a real, checkable distinction worth confirming for any specific company or program before signing up with one. The two named case studies (a 21-year-old earning $1,200 a month from a single four-year-contracted account, another scaling to $60,000 a month) are specific but unverified personal claims, and the presenter is candid that it took roughly 600 rejected pitches before the first real deal, a genuine transparency signal about how slow the early stage can be.

Our assessment, based on the video and what’s typical across similar videos. We don’t independently verify income claims or prices.
