Acrylic Awards With a CO2 Laser

Engraved acrylic awards, plaques and desk name plates for companies, schools and nonprofits made on a K40 CO2 laser

What It Is

The K40 is an inexpensive imported CO2 laser that, unlike most diode lasers, cuts and engraves clear acrylic with smooth, flame-polished edges and also engraves anodized aluminum, leather, rubber and coated metals. The main product is what award shops sell: acrylic awards, engraved plaques, employee-of-the-month and retirement awards, sales trophies and desk name plates, for companies, school banquets, brokerages, hospitals, police and fire departments and nonprofits. A crystal-look acrylic award costs a few dollars in material while award shops commonly charge $40 to $100 or more. It also makes acrylic wedding table numbers, lobby signs and rubber stamps.

The Pitch

The creator calls it the machine trophy shops would rather you never heard of: a $400 box that competes with an industry charging premium prices for decades. He points out that every company, school and nonprofit holds an annual awards event.

Who It’s Best For

Technically minded makers willing to learn a demanding machine safely, who can reach HR managers, schools and organizations that buy awards.

The Truth (as we see it)

Engraved acrylic awards are a real, recurring market and a CO2 laser is the right tool for clear acrylic. The creator is frank that the K40 has high voltage inside, minimal stock safety features and can start fires; it needs ventilation, water cooling, a lid switch, air assist and a fire extinguisher, and must never be left running or used on PVC or vinyl. Budget time and money for those upgrades. Award-shop prices are his estimates. The video also promotes a collection of side-hustle guides through an on-screen QR code.
