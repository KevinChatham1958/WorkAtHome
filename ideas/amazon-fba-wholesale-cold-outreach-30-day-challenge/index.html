Amazon FBA Wholesale Challenge

Amazon FBA wholesale: cold-outreach brand acquisition (30-day documented challenge)

Abuv The Par · I Tried Amazon FBA For 30 Days to Prove It's Not Dead ↗

What It Is

A documented 30-day challenge building the same brand-direct Amazon wholesale model from zero, with the actual cold-outreach mechanics spelled out: source at least 100 target brands (under $3 million/month in sales, already selling on Amazon, not huge household names) using SmartScout, find the decision-maker's direct contact via AI tools or LinkedIn, then email no more than 30 brands a day to protect your sending domain from spam flags. Track open rate (aim for around 30%; below that, fix the subject line) and reply rate (aim for 3-5%; below that, fix the email body) separately, and follow up on every 'no' with a 'value stack' document showing the brand what its competitors are earning on Amazon that it isn't.

The Pitch

The presenter's stated real averages: out of 100 outreach emails, about 2-3 brands convert into a wholesale account; opening one typically costs around $8,000 in inventory and returns roughly $16,000/month in revenue and $2,400-3,000/month in net profit once live, with a real timeline of about 14-21 days from first email to the product actually selling on Amazon. The core argument is that most people who call Amazon FBA 'dead' quit after one round of mediocre cold emails rather than iterating the message, and that a brand's 'no' — not its 'yes' — is where the real opportunity sits, since a no usually means no one has offered that brand real value yet either.

Who It’s Best For

Someone with a few thousand dollars in working capital and the discipline to run a real cold-email campaign — writing, testing, and systematically following up on rejections — rather than a one-and-done pitch.

The Truth (as we see it)

This is a self-documented challenge by the channel owner, with the presenter's own claimed $15 million in prior-year sales and other creators' results (a claimed $52,000 first month, someone leaving a six-figure job within six months) offered as unverified anecdotes to build credibility. The 30-day window is presented with an honest caveat: the reported profit figures only reflect about two weeks of live sales data within the 30 days, since inventory takes time to actually go live on Amazon, so the real monthly run rate would be higher than what's shown. Real numbers and a real timeline are a plus, but they come from one insider's own operation, not an independently audited case.
