One-Skill Digital Product

First digital product built from one skill you already have (guide, templates or checklist pack)

Digital & subscription products · Near-zero cost (your time; a blog page or simple order form to sell)

What It Is

You take one skill people already ask you for help with (designing posts, writing captions, cooking, teaching a subject, managing a shop's social media) and package it into a small product you can sell many times: a short practical guide, a set of ready-made templates, a checklist pack or a short video series. Before building, you check demand by asking a group, your status followers or existing customers what confuses them most; if around 10 people name the same problem, that becomes the topic. You build it in a focused weekend from an outline (the problem, the steps, the finished result, mistakes to avoid) using your own examples and screenshots. You price it in line with similar guides or template packs in your market and sell it through a WhatsApp status post, a blog page or a simple order form, launching first to about 10 people who already asked you for help and updating it from their feedback.

The Pitch

The creator says it lets you stop selling your time over and over and instead sell the same product more than once. He argues that one finished small product beats a long list of half-done ideas, and that checking demand first is the step beginners skip that saves the most time. Starting with your first 10 buyers, he says, gives you real feedback that makes the second launch stronger.

Who It's Best For

People with one clear, practical skill and a small existing network (customers, classmates, followers) who can write, design or explain things clearly.

The Truth (as we see it)

This is sober, low-risk advice with no income promises, and the creator explicitly warns against fake reviews, fake earnings screenshots and copying other people's work. The method is generic, though: it gives no numbers on what sells, what to charge or how to find buyers beyond your own contacts, so expect slow, small sales at first. Selling through status posts and direct messages works for a warm audience but does not scale without a real channel such as a marketplace, email list or content. The creator does not pitch a paid product here; he only points to his free blog and social accounts.
