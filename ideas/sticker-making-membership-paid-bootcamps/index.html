Craft Membership via Paid Bootcamps

Online sticker-making membership for crafters, filled through low-cost paid live bootcamps

  • Digital products
  • $100–$500 to start
  • Jump to The Truth ↓

Video published Jul 10, 2026 by Alex Hormozi

Startup cost: Low to moderate startup cost (membership platform, recorded lessons and craft supplies; growing it runs on Meta ads) (our estimate; the video doesn’t give figures)

Alex Hormozi · Building a $3,000,000 Business for a Stranger in 31 Minutes | Scale or Fail ↗

What It Is

You teach a craft skill online, here making stickers, through a monthly membership of pre-recorded classes, sold at $27 a month or $217 a year. New members come in through a live paid bootcamp run three times a year: people pay $10 for a ticket, you go live every day of the event, and you pitch the membership at the end, with lower-priced digital products as upsells. The featured owner's numbers: about $37 in ad cost per bootcamp buyer, about $36 average order value, 22% of buyers joining the membership, roughly $300 lifetime value per member and about 5% monthly churn. Hormozi's advice is to offer only the annual plan during launches with a physical sticker kit thrown in, test the bootcamp as a free trial of the membership, and make far more ads, including clips from her lives and videos of members making their first sticker.

The Pitch

The owner started it as a creative outlet during cancer treatment 10 years ago, says she has taught over 20,000 crafters and made about AU$1.1 million (roughly US$800,000) in the last 12 months at a 30 to 35% profit, with two employees, one of them her husband. Hormozi calls it a simple model to scale because doubling the number of members doesn't strain delivery; the only question is whether ads can bring people in for less than they are worth.

Who It’s Best For

Skilled hobbyists who enjoy teaching on camera and are willing to learn paid advertising and run launch events several times a year.

The Truth (as we see it)

This is a real, proven type of home business, and the video is unusually open about launch costs and conversion rates. All figures are the owner's own and unaudited, and they reflect 10 years of list-building and marketing experience; a newcomer should expect early launches to lose money on ads before breaking even, as hers sometimes did. The approach depends on being comfortable on camera and on running Meta ads well, and craft teaching online is crowded. The episode is part of Hormozi's Scale or Fail show for Acquisition.com, where he decides whether to work with the owner for a year; she mentions using his ads playbook, and other episodes of the show promote his free roadmap and paid workshops.
