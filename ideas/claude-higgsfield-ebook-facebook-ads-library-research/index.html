Upgraded Ebooks From the Ads Library

Finding proven-selling ebooks via the Facebook Ads Library, then rebuilding and upgrading them with Claude AI connected to an image/video generation tool

Digital & subscription products · Low (a Gumroad account, an AI image/video generation subscription, a Facebook ad budget starting around $10/day)

What It Is

Use the free, public Facebook Ads Library (filtered to a strong ad-spending country and to ads running 5+ days, a signal of profitability) to build a list of proven-selling digital products, feed that list to Claude to identify the strongest candidate; rather than copying, connect Claude to an AI image/video tool via MCP and use a prompt sequence to analyze the original, generate an upgraded positioning and table of contents, write the ebook chapter-by-chapter with AI-generated images, and produce cover options, merging everything into a finished PDF; publish on Gumroad with a discount coupon, then use the same connected tools to generate video/image ad creatives and a suggested target audience, running a structured two-ad-set Facebook test (with and without interest targeting).

The Pitch

The presenter shows real, unedited results: after finding a proven-selling hormone-health ebook via the Ads Library and rebuilding an upgraded version, he spent $60 on Facebook ads and made $124 back (~$64 profit) on an incomplete test, explicitly showing one ad set burned money with zero results while the other converted, and demonstrates a free alternative (theme pages on Instagram/TikTok) for those without ad budget.

Who It's Best For

Someone comfortable chaining AI tools via connectors/MCP and willing to run and interpret a real paid ad test rather than expecting guaranteed results.

The Truth (as we see it)

The Facebook Ads Library is a real, free, publicly available research tool, and AI tools connecting via MCP for content creation is a real, current capability demonstrated with real screen recordings. The presenter's own results are modest and transparent rather than an exaggerated success story, though this is a single, early-stage, unfinished test.
