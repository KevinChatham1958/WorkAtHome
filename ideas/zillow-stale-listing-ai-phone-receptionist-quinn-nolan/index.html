Realtor AI Phone Receptionist

AI phone and text receptionist for real estate agents' listings, pitched to agents with homes sitting unsold on Zillow

  • $100–$500 to start

What It Is

You use Zillow's free 'find an agent' search to pick agents in a city, look for listings that have sat for months or had price cuts, and get the agent's contact details from their profile or website. Each agent gets a personal email naming their listing, with a 60-second screen recording of you looking at it, offering a free AI bot for that home. In GoHighLevel you paste the listing details into its AI builder to create a voice agent that answers buyer calls, gives facts like bedrooms and price, and books showings on a calendar, plus a matching text-message agent; an optional daily automation pulls new and sold listings from a free third-party real estate data API. After a free trial of about a week or 10 calls, you charge a monthly fee, which he puts at $297 to $400.

The Pitch

The creator says agents only get paid when a home sells and most have no assistant answering calls around the clock, so a bot that books showings fills a real gap that a CRM's follow-up texts do not. He says agent contact details are easy to find for free, setup takes about five minutes, and monthly billing builds recurring revenue. He reports 12 new customers in about 45 days on a $400-a-month version, and says he got his first $1,100 payment years ago by sending 20 personal messages a day.

Who It’s Best For

People comfortable with cold outreach and with setting up and supporting software for small-business clients, who want recurring monthly income.

The Truth (as we see it)

Selling AI receptionists to local businesses is a common pitch among WAH creators, and the real-estate angle is a sensible fit because agents do lose calls. The customer numbers and the '$500 a day' headline are his own claims and unaudited; real results depend on many personal pitches, closing sales, and keeping clients after the free trial. He promotes his GoHighLevel affiliate link, offers a paid upgrade and a 'school' community, and gives away his build in exchange for likes and comments. He warns that listing data from the third-party API is not the agent's MLS and should be checked with their broker, and you would also be answering for any wrong information the bot gives callers.

Our assessment, based on the video and what’s typical across similar videos. We don’t independently verify income claims or prices.
