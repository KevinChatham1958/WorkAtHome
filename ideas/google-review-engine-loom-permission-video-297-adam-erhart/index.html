Google Review Automation Service

Google review automation service for local businesses, sold with a permission-based 2-minute Loom video ($297/month)

What It Is

You sell local service businesses an automated system that asks every finished customer for a Google review by text, sends one reminder unless they already clicked, and auto-replies to new reviews. Prospects are found on Google Maps: well-rated businesses (for example a garage door company with 4.8 stars and 73 reviews) that trail competitors with hundreds. You send a short email or DM asking permission to share a free 2-minute video; only when they say yes do you record a Loom screen walkthrough of their listing with one free fix (replying to their existing reviews). When they ask for help, you offer the setup for $297 a month, built in HighLevel.

The Pitch

The creator says this avoids cold calls and sales calls because the owner asks to see the video and can see the problem on his own Google listing. He expects about 1 in 10 to 1 in 20 messages to get a yes to the video, and says each client takes about 30 minutes a month to manage once set up, so 10 clients is just under $3,000 a month. He is open that getting clients is the hard part and that most people quit after a few unanswered messages.

Who It’s Best For

Someone comfortable sending 10-20 personalized messages a day and following up patiently, who wants a simple recurring service they can run alone from home.

The Truth (as we see it)

Review request automation is a real, widely sold service, and the outreach approach (lead with a specific, visible problem, ask permission before sending anything) is more respectful and more likely to work than generic pitches. The creator is careful to warn against review gating, which breaks Google's rules. The reply rates and income math are his own estimates, and many local review tools compete on price, so $297 a month may be hard to hold. The video promotes HighLevel through his affiliate link, along with his own 'AgencyOS' bundle and a free masterclass that leads into his paid offers, and the push toward the top HighLevel plan benefits him as an affiliate.

Our assessment, based on the video and what’s typical across similar videos. We don’t independently verify income claims or prices.
