What It Is

Design a business's complete visual identity (logo, color palette, typography, imagery style) as a full package rather than a single deliverable like a logo, aimed at businesses that want to look immediately recognizable and trustworthy to customers.

The Pitch

Tilbury cites Jack Watson, who went from a negative bank balance to his first $20,000 month within six months of specializing in this. The pitch leans on a clear market gap: there are many designers charging around $50 for a quick logo, but few offering the complete, cohesive package that makes a brand feel genuinely professional, which is why brand identity is positioned as a premium, underserved tier of graphic design. As evidence of real market size, the video cites the global brand identity market valued at over $9.5 billion in 2024, projected to reach $40 billion by 2032, and a real example of the UK government reportedly spending roughly $650,000 on a single logo redesign.

Who It’s Best For

Someone with genuine visual design skill and taste who's willing to develop a full-package offering rather than staying at the cheaper, more commoditized single-logo tier of the market.

The Truth (as we see it)

The market-size figures ($9.5 billion in 2024, projected $40 billion by 2032) and the government logo redesign cost are specific, checkable claims, though the market-size numbers' original source isn't named in the video. The Jack Watson case study, like the others in this video, is featured because he's an instructor on the presenter's own paid course platform, worth factoring in when weighing how the results are framed. The underlying market gap described (cheap single-logo work vs. a full cohesive identity package) is a genuine, recognizable distinction in the design industry, independent of any specific case study's numbers.

Our assessment, based on the video and what’s typical across similar videos. We don’t independently verify income claims or prices.
