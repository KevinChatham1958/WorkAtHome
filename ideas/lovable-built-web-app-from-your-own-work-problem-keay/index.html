AI-built Web App or App-plus-service Business Solving a Problem From Your Own Work (Lovable)

Digital & subscription products · Very low startup cost (Lovable free tier or about $25/month plan)

Silicon Valley Girl · $13B Lovable Co-Founder: Build a Profitable AI Product From Scratch in 30 Minutes ↗

What It Is

You take a problem you know closely from your own job or life and build a working web app for it by describing it to Lovable, which handles the code, hosting, data, email and payments. In the video, the co-founder builds an inbound-opportunity inbox for the host that captures requests, scores them with AI and drafts replies, and runs end-to-end and security checks before publishing. He suggests pairing software with a real-world service, citing a site that turns customers' dog photos into painted-style portraits and ships them as prints. You talk to about 10 potential customers, get at least one to pay, and only then work on scaling marketing.

The Pitch

The co-founder says more than a third of people building businesses on Lovable already make money, that most have more than 11 years of work experience and are building for problems they faced themselves, and that the dog-portrait site makes about $300,000 a month. He argues that as building gets easier, the idea and creativity become the main differentiator, and that charging from the start shows whether people truly care.

Best For

Experienced professionals who have a specific, recurring problem in their field and are willing to talk to potential customers.

The Truth

This is an interview with the co-founder of the tool being demonstrated, so it is naturally promotional for Lovable; the video is also sponsored by HubSpot for Startups. Platform-wide statistics and the $300,000 a month example come from the company and are not independently verified. The advice is general (talk to customers, charge early, find distribution) and the demo is a personal tool rather than a business. Building is now the easy part; finding paying customers and marketing remain the hard parts, as the co-founder acknowledges.
