Long-Haul Expert Podcast Brand

Expert content brand from zero followers: years of weekly podcasts and daily short-form posts plus daily outreach

Alex Hormozi · How to Grow an Audience if You Have 0 Followers ↗

What It Is

You start publishing about what you already know from your work and commit to it for years, not months. Hormozi's own podcast began in 2017 with about 150 episodes on how to run a gym better before widening to marketing, sales and business, with one to three episodes every week for six years until it reached the top 10. His second example is a friend who sold his gym chain, then posted short-form videos every day for six months and paired them with daily direct outreach to sell, until videos took off and he was making about $100,000 a month. The daily measure of success is whether you posted and did your outreach, not the results.

The Pitch

Hormozi argues people quit because they expect to blow up in 90 days, when the first year is just finding your voice. If you make 400 long-form pieces, he says, your following will be bigger than it is today, and once you can keep doing the work without seeing results, 'money will never be a constraint in your life.'

Who It’s Best For

Experienced professionals or business owners with real know-how who can keep publishing for years without quick results.

The Truth (as we see it)

This video was published in 2022, more than two years ago, so platform tactics and the numbers may have changed. It is mainly a talk on consistency: the steady-output advice is sound, but it doesn't say what the friend sold to earn $100,000 a month, and both examples involve people who had already run gyms for years. Hormozi had an established business before his podcast took off, and the video offers no plan for what to sell, so expect years of unpaid work. He sells books and runs Acquisition.com, though this short clip doesn't pitch them.

Our assessment, based on the video and what’s typical across similar videos. We don’t independently verify income claims or prices.

What Viewers Say

Comments mostly agree with the video's message of steady daily posting, and several creators share how slowly their own channels grew at first before picking up. There's little pushback; one viewer jokes that the whole method is to post constantly and wait years.
