White-Label Local Business Software

White-label texting, reviews and text-to-pay software resold to local businesses (cloning a funded competitor)

Marketing & content · Moderate startup cost (white-label software subscription after a 30-day trial)

Originally published Oct 16, 2026 — Jason Wardrop

Share on Facebook Link copied
Source

Jason Wardrop · How I Make $500 Per Day Copy & Paste (How To Make Money Online 2026) ↗

What It Is

You pick a funded software company that sells simple tools to small local businesses and offer the same features under your own brand. The creator walks through a pre-built white-label platform with two-way business texting, review requests (reputation management), a website chat widget, text-to-pay invoicing, missed-call text-back, a CRM and a brandable mobile app. You set your own monthly plans in a 'SaaS configurator' (his examples are roughly $249, $399 and $449 a month), swap your logo into a pre-made website and demo video, and sign up local businesses such as plumbers and electricians. Income is the monthly subscription each client pays, minus the platform fee.

The Pitch

The creator says the model is proven because a company selling these exact tools has made a fast-growth list three years running, and because you sell software rather than hours, revenue stacks month to month instead of resetting. He says 100 clients at $500 a month would be $50,000 in monthly recurring revenue, and shows dashboards he says total over $200,000 in monthly recurring revenue.

Who It's Best For

People comfortable selling to small business owners who want a recurring-revenue business and are willing to handle client onboarding and support.

The Truth (as we see it)

Reselling white-label marketing software to local businesses is a real and widely taught model, and the feature set described is genuinely useful to service businesses. The revenue screenshots and the $50,000-a-month example are the creator's own, unaudited, and the math assumes no client cancellations, which is unrealistic for small-business software. He says he has nothing to sell, but the free course is gated behind a comment, and the extended 30-day trial of the software comes through his link, which commonly means an affiliate commission; the platform itself has an ongoing monthly fee after the trial. The hard part, which the video largely skips, is finding and closing clients and supporting them once they sign up.
